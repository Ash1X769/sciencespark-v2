## SECTION 8.1 — Pure Substances and Formulations

---

**C8-01** | Topic: Purity | Tier: F

Q: What is a **pure substance** in chemistry? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A substance made of only **one element or compound** — nothing else mixed in
- Has a **specific, sharp melting point** and boiling point

Hint: Pure = one substance only. Not mixed with anything. Sharp melting point confirms it.

Exam Q: "How can you use melting point to tell if a substance is pure?" [2 marks]
→ Pure substance has a sharp / specific melting point (1) + impure substance melts over a range of temperatures (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-purity

---

**C8-02** | Topic: Purity | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **impurities** affect melting point.

A:
- Impurities **lower** the melting point of a substance
- Impurities cause melting to occur over a **range** of temperatures — not a sharp point

Hint: Impure = lower + broader melting range. Pure = sharp, specific temperature.

Exam Q: "A student finds a substance melts between 48°C and 53°C. What does this suggest about the substance?" [2 marks]
→ The substance is impure (1) + impurities lower and broaden the melting point / melts over a range (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-purity

---

**C8-03** | Topic: Formulations | Tier: F

Q: What is a **formulation**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **mixture** that has been carefully designed as a **useful product**
- Made by mixing components in **precisely measured quantities**

Hint: Formulations are designed mixtures — not random. Medicines, paints, fuels, cleaning products.

Exam Q: "What is meant by a formulation?" [2 marks]
→ A mixture designed as a useful product (1) + components mixed in carefully measured quantities (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-formulations

---

**C8-04** | Topic: Formulations | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 examples** of **formulations**.

A:
- **Medicines** — precise amounts of active ingredients
- **Paints** — pigment, solvent, binder in specific proportions
- **Fuels** — mixtures of hydrocarbons
- **Cleaning products**, **fertilisers**, **foods**

Hint: Any carefully designed mixture product = formulation. Most everyday products are formulations.

Exam Q: "Give two examples of formulations." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: medicines / paints / fuels / foods / fertilisers / cleaning products / alloys.

## SECTION 8.2 — Chromatography

---

**C8-05** | Topic: Chromatography | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **paper chromatography**.

A:
- Used to **separate and identify** components of a mixture
- Components travel different distances depending on how strongly they're **attracted to the paper** vs the **solvent**
- Each substance has a characteristic **Rf value** that can identify it

Hint: Different substances travel different distances. Rf value identifies each one.

Exam Q: "What is chromatography used for?" [2 marks]
→ Separate mixtures (1) + identify substances in a mixture (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-chromatography

---

**C8-06** | Topic: Chromatography | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 steps** for carrying out **paper chromatography**.

A:
- Draw a line in **pencil** near the bottom of the paper (not pen — pen ink would dissolve)
- Place **spots** of the mixture on the pencil line
- Place the paper in **solvent** — below the pencil line — and allow the solvent to travel up

Hint: Pencil line (not pen) → spots on line → solvent runs up → components separate.

Exam Q: "Describe how to carry out paper chromatography to separate a mixture of dyes." [3 marks]
→ Pencil line at base (1) + spots of mixture placed on line (1) + placed in solvent below the line (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-chromatography

---

**C8-07** | Topic: Chromatography | Tier: F

Q: What are the **stationary phase** and **mobile phase** in chromatography? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Stationary phase** — the medium the sample moves through (e.g. the **paper**)
- **Mobile phase** — the **solvent** that carries the sample up through the stationary phase

Hint: Stationary = stays still (paper). Mobile = moves (solvent).

Exam Q: "What is the stationary phase in paper chromatography?" [1 mark]
→ The paper.

Notes link: /notes/c8-chromatography

---

**C8-08** | Topic: Chromatography | Tier: F

Q: What is the **Rf value** and how is it calculated? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A value that shows how far a substance has moved compared to the solvent
- **Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent front**

Hint: Rf is always between 0 and 1. Spot distance ÷ solvent distance.

Exam Q: "A substance travels 4.5 cm. The solvent front travels 9 cm. Calculate the Rf value." [1 mark]
→ Rf = 4.5 ÷ 9 = **0.5**.

Notes link: /notes/c8-chromatography

---

**C8-09** | Topic: Chromatography | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **Rf values** are used to identify substances.

A:
- Each substance has a **characteristic Rf value** in a given solvent
- Unknown substances can be **identified** by comparing their Rf values to **known** values

Hint: Match the Rf value to known data = identify the substance. Like a fingerprint.

Exam Q: "How can chromatography be used to identify an unknown substance?" [2 marks]
→ Calculate the Rf value (1) + compare to known Rf values of substances (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-chromatography

## SECTION 8.3 — Testing for Gases

---

**C8-10** | Topic: Gas Tests | Tier: F

Q: Give the **test** and **positive result** for **hydrogen**.

A:
- Hold a **lit splint** near the gas
- Positive result: a **squeaky pop** sound as the hydrogen burns

Hint: Squeaky pop = hydrogen. The gas burns explosively with oxygen in the air.

Exam Q: "Describe the test for hydrogen gas and state the positive result." [2 marks]
→ Lit splint held near gas (1) + squeaky pop (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-gas-tests

---

**C8-11** | Topic: Gas Tests | Tier: F

Q: Give the **test** and **positive result** for **oxygen**.

A:
- Hold a **glowing splint** in the gas
- Positive result: the splint **relights**

Hint: Glowing splint relights = oxygen. Oxygen supports combustion.

Exam Q: "Describe the test for oxygen gas and state the positive result." [2 marks]
→ Glowing splint held in gas (1) + splint relights (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-gas-tests

---

**C8-12** | Topic: Gas Tests | Tier: F

Q: Give the **test** and **positive result** for **carbon dioxide**.

A:
- Bubble the gas through **limewater** (calcium hydroxide solution)
- Positive result: limewater turns **milky / cloudy**

Hint: CO₂ + limewater = milky. Goes cloudy = carbon dioxide confirmed.

Exam Q: "Describe the test for carbon dioxide gas and state the positive result." [2 marks]
→ Bubble through limewater (1) + limewater turns milky / cloudy (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-gas-tests

---

**C8-13** | Topic: Gas Tests | Tier: F

Q: Give the **test** and **positive result** for **chlorine**.

A:
- Hold **damp litmus paper** near the gas
- Positive result: the litmus paper is **bleached white**

Hint: Chlorine bleaches damp litmus white. It's also a pale green gas with a sharp smell.

Exam Q: "Describe the test for chlorine gas and state the positive result." [2 marks]
→ Damp litmus paper held near gas (1) + bleached white (1).

Notes link: /notes/c8-gas-tests
