## SECTION 7.1 — Crude Oil and Hydrocarbons

---

**C7-01** | Topic: Crude Oil | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **crude oil**.

A:
- A **mixture** of many different **hydrocarbons**
- Formed over millions of years from **dead plankton** subjected to heat and pressure
- A **finite** resource — it will eventually run out

Hint: Dead sea creatures + millions of years + heat and pressure = crude oil. Non-renewable.

Exam Q: "What is crude oil?" [2 marks]
→ A mixture of hydrocarbons (1) + formed from remains of ancient organisms over millions of years (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-crude-oil

---

**C7-02** | Topic: Crude Oil | Tier: F

Q: What is a **hydrocarbon**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A compound containing **only** carbon and hydrogen atoms
- Bonded together by **covalent** bonds

Hint: Hydro = hydrogen. Carbon = carbon. Only these two elements — nothing else.

Exam Q: "What is a hydrocarbon?" [1 mark]
→ A compound containing only carbon and hydrogen atoms.

## SECTION 7.2 — Alkanes

---

**C7-03** | Topic: Alkanes | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **alkanes**.

A:
- General formula: **CₙH₂ₙ₊₂**
- Contain only **single** C−C bonds — they are **saturated**
- Do **not** react with bromine water — bromine water stays **orange/brown**

Hint: Saturated = only single bonds. No reaction with bromine water = alkane test.

Exam Q: "What is the general formula for alkanes?" [1 mark]
→ CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

Notes link: /notes/c7-alkanes

---

**C7-04** | Topic: Alkanes | Tier: F

Q: Give the **name and formula** of the first **4 alkanes**.

A:
- **Methane** — CH₄
- **Ethane** — C₂H₆
- **Propane** — C₃H₈
- **Butane** — C₄H₁₀

Hint: Meth(1), Eth(2), Prop(3), But(4). Apply CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ to get the formula.

Exam Q: "Give the molecular formula of propane." [1 mark]
→ C₃H₈.

## SECTION 7.3 — Fractional Distillation

---

**C7-05** | Topic: Fractional Distillation | Tier: F

Q: Give **5 steps** of **fractional distillation** of crude oil.

A:
- Crude oil is **heated** to around 400°C — most of it **vaporises**
- Vapours rise up a **fractionating column** which is cooler at the top
- Each fraction **condenses** when it cools to its **boiling point**
- Fractions with **higher boiling points** condense **lower** down
- Fractions with **lower boiling points** rise **higher** before condensing

Hint: Heat → vapour rises → cools → condenses at its boiling point → collected.

Exam Q: "Describe how fractional distillation separates crude oil." [4 marks]
→ Crude oil heated / vaporises (1) + vapours rise up column (1) + cool as they rise (1) + condense at boiling point / different levels (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-fractional-distillation

---

**C7-06** | Topic: Fractional Distillation | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 fractions** from crude oil, their **boiling points** and **uses**.

A:
- **Petrol** — low boiling point — fuel for cars
- **Kerosene** (jet fuel) — medium boiling point — fuel for aircraft
- **Diesel** — higher boiling point — fuel for lorries
- **Heavy fuel oil** — highest boiling point — fuel for ships / power stations

Hint: Higher up the column = shorter chains = lower boiling point = more flammable.

Exam Q: "Name one fraction obtained from crude oil and give its use." [2 marks]
→ Any 1 fraction (1) + correct use (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-fractional-distillation

---

**C7-07** | Topic: Fractional Distillation | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 trends** in properties going **up** the fractionating column.

A:
- **Boiling point decreases** — shorter chains, weaker intermolecular forces
- **Flammability increases** — shorter chains burn more easily
- **Viscosity decreases** — shorter chains flow more easily

Hint: Up the column = shorter chains = lower boiling point, more flammable, less viscous (runnier).

Exam Q: "Give one difference between petrol and heavy fuel oil." [1 mark]
→ Any 1 from: petrol has shorter chains / lower boiling point / more flammable / less viscous.

Notes link: /notes/c7-fractional-distillation

---

**C7-08** | Topic: Fractional Distillation | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about **intermolecular forces** and why they affect boiling point.

A:
- Longer hydrocarbon chains have **stronger intermolecular forces** between molecules
- More energy is needed to overcome them → **higher boiling point**

Hint: Longer chain = stronger forces between molecules = harder to boil = higher boiling point.

Exam Q: "Explain why longer chain hydrocarbons have higher boiling points." [2 marks]
→ Stronger intermolecular forces (1) + more energy needed to overcome them (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-fractional-distillation

## SECTION 7.4 — Combustion

---

**C7-09** | Topic: Combustion | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **complete combustion** of hydrocarbons.

A:
- Occurs when there is **sufficient oxygen**
- Products: **carbon dioxide** and **water**
- Releases **more energy** than incomplete combustion

Hint: Enough oxygen → CO₂ + water. Clean combustion.

Exam Q: "What are the products of complete combustion of a hydrocarbon fuel?" [2 marks]
→ Carbon dioxide (1) + water (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-combustion

---

**C7-10** | Topic: Combustion | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **incomplete combustion** of hydrocarbons.

A:
- Occurs when there is **insufficient oxygen**
- Products: **carbon monoxide (CO)** and/or **soot** (carbon particles) and water
- Carbon monoxide is **toxic** — colourless and odourless so hard to detect

Hint: Not enough oxygen → CO and/or soot (carbon) + water. Dangerous.

Exam Q: "Give two products of incomplete combustion of a hydrocarbon." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: carbon monoxide / carbon / soot / water.

Notes link: /notes/c7-combustion

---

**C7-11** | Topic: Combustion | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about why **carbon monoxide** is dangerous.

A:
- It is **toxic** — can cause death even in small concentrations
- It is **colourless and odourless** — very hard to detect without a monitor

Hint: You can't see it or smell it. It's produced by incomplete combustion in boilers and fires.

Exam Q: "Give two reasons why carbon monoxide is particularly dangerous." [2 marks]
→ Toxic / can cause death (1) + colourless and odourless / hard to detect (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-combustion

## SECTION 7.5 — Alkenes

---

**C7-12** | Topic: Alkenes | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **alkenes**.

A:
- General formula: **CₙH₂ₙ**
- Contain at least one **C=C double bond** — they are **unsaturated**
- **Decolourise** bromine water — orange/brown → colourless

Hint: Unsaturated = double bond. Bromine water goes colourless = alkene present.

Exam Q: "What is the general formula for alkenes?" [1 mark]
→ CₙH₂ₙ.

Notes link: /notes/c7-alkenes

---

**C7-13** | Topic: Alkenes | Tier: F

Q: Give the **name and formula** of the first **2 alkenes**.

A:
- **Ethene** — C₂H₄
- **Propene** — C₃H₆

Hint: Eth(2) + ene = ethene C₂H₄. Prop(3) + ene = propene C₃H₆.

Exam Q: "Give the molecular formula of ethene." [1 mark]
→ C₂H₄.

Notes link: /notes/c7-alkenes

---

**C7-14** | Topic: Alkenes | Tier: F

Q: What is the **test for an alkene**? Give the result. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Add **bromine water** (orange/brown colour)
- **Alkene present**: turns **colourless** — addition reaction occurs across the double bond
- **Alkane present**: bromine water stays **orange/brown** — no reaction

Hint: Orange → colourless = alkene. Stays orange = alkane.

Exam Q: "Describe the test to distinguish between an alkane and an alkene." [2 marks]
→ Add bromine water (1) + alkene decolourises it / alkane has no effect (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-alkenes

---

**C7-15** | Topic: Alkenes | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about **addition reactions** of alkenes.

A:
- The **C=C double bond** opens up
- A small molecule **adds across** the double bond — producing **one single product**

Hint: Double bond opens + something adds across = one product. e.g. ethene + bromine → dibromoethane.

Exam Q: "Describe the addition reaction of ethene with bromine." [2 marks]
→ Double bond opens up (1) + bromine adds across / one product formed (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-alkenes

## SECTION 7.6 — Cracking

---

**C7-16** | Topic: Cracking | Tier: F

Q: What is **cracking**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Breaking down **long-chain** hydrocarbons into **shorter-chain** ones
- Always produces an **alkene** as well as shorter alkanes
- The atoms in the products are the **same** as in the reactants — conservation of mass

Hint: Long chain snapped into shorter ones. Always makes an alkene. Atoms rearrange, never lost.

Exam Q: "What is cracking?" [2 marks]
→ Breaking down long-chain hydrocarbons (1) + into shorter, more useful molecules (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-cracking

---

**C7-17** | Topic: Cracking | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 methods** of **cracking** and their conditions.

A:
- **Catalytic cracking** — heated in the presence of a **zeolite catalyst** at around **500°C**
- **Steam cracking** — fraction is diluted with **steam** and heated to around **850°C**

Hint: Catalyst cracking (lower temp, catalyst). Steam cracking (higher temp, steam, no catalyst).

Exam Q: "Describe the conditions used in catalytic cracking." [2 marks]
→ Zeolite catalyst (1) + approximately 500°C / high temperature (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-cracking

---

**C7-18** | Topic: Cracking | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 reasons** why **cracking** is important.

A:
- Converts **less useful** long-chain fractions into **more useful** shorter-chain fuels
- Produces **alkenes** which are needed to make **polymers** (plastics)
- Helps meet the high **demand** for petrol and other fuels

Hint: More fuel + monomers for plastics from less useful heavy fractions = economically vital.

Exam Q: "Give two reasons why cracking is important in the petroleum industry." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: produces useful fuels / produces alkenes for polymers / meets demand for petrol.

Notes link: /notes/c7-cracking

## SECTION 7.7 — Polymers

---

**C7-19** | Topic: Polymers | Tier: F

Q: What is **addition polymerisation**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Many small **monomer** molecules (alkenes) join together
- The **C=C double bonds** open up and monomers link in a chain
- Forms one large **polymer** molecule — no other products

Hint: Alkene monomers → double bonds open → join up → polymer only. No waste.

Exam Q: "Describe addition polymerisation." [3 marks]
→ Alkene monomers with double bonds (1) + double bonds open / monomers join (1) + one product / polymer formed (1).

Notes link: /notes/c7-polymers

---

**C7-20** | Topic: Polymers | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about problems with **disposal of polymers**.

A:
- Most polymers are **non-biodegradable** — don't break down in the environment
- Contribute to **plastic pollution** in oceans and landfill

Hint: Plastics persist for hundreds of years. Huge environmental problem.

Exam Q: "Give one problem with the disposal of polymers." [1 mark]
→ Non-biodegradable / persist in environment / contribute to pollution.
