## SECTION 2.1 — Key Terms

---

**P2-01** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **electric current**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **flow of electric charge**
- Measured in **amperes (A)** using an **ammeter** connected in **series**

Hint: Current = charge flowing. More charge per second = higher current.

Exam Q: "What is electric current?" [1 mark]
→ The flow of electric charge.

Notes link: /notes/p2-key-terms

---

**P2-02** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **potential difference (voltage)**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **work done** per unit charge between two points in a circuit
- It is what **drives** the charge around the circuit
- Measured in **volts (V)** using a **voltmeter** connected in **parallel**

Hint: Potential difference = the "push" that drives current. More PD = more energy per charge.

Exam Q: "What is potential difference?" [2 marks]
→ Work done per unit charge / energy transferred per unit charge (1) + drives charge around the circuit (1).

Notes link: /notes/p2-key-terms

---

**P2-03** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **resistance**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Anything that **opposes the flow** of electric charge
- Measured in **ohms (Ω)**

Hint: Resistance = opposition to current. More resistance = less current for same voltage.

Exam Q: "What is electrical resistance?" [1 mark]
→ Opposition to the flow of electric charge / current.

## SECTION 2.2 — Key Equations

---

**P2-04** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation for **charge flow**? *(1 fact)*

A:
- **Charge flow = current × time**
- **Q = It**
- Units: charge in **coulombs (C)**, current in **A**, time in **s**

Hint: Q = It. More current × more time = more charge flows.

Exam Q: "A current of 3 A flows for 10 s. Calculate the charge flow." [2 marks]
→ Q = 3 × 10 = **30 C**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p2-equations

---

**P2-05** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is **Ohm's Law**? Give the equation. *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Potential difference = current × resistance**
- **V = IR**
- Units: V in **volts (V)**, I in **amperes (A)**, R in **ohms (Ω)**

Hint: V = IR. Voltage equals Current times Resistance.

Exam Q: "Calculate the resistance of a component with a current of 2 A and a potential difference of 12 V." [2 marks]
→ R = V ÷ I = 12 ÷ 2 = **6 Ω**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p2-equations

---

**P2-06** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 equations** for **electrical power**.

A:
- **Power = potential difference × current** → **P = VI**
- **Power = current² × resistance** → **P = I²R**
- Units: power in **W**, V in **V**, I in **A**, R in **Ω**

Hint: P = VI and P = I²R. Use whichever values you're given.

Exam Q: "Calculate the power of a component with a current of 3 A and resistance of 4 Ω." [2 marks]
→ P = I²R = 3² × 4 = 9 × 4 = **36 W**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p2-equations

---

**P2-07** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 equations** for **energy transferred** electrically.

A:
- **Energy transferred = power × time** → **E = Pt**
- **Energy transferred = charge flow × potential difference** → **E = QV**
- Units: energy in **J**, power in **W**, time in **s**, charge in **C**, V in **V**

Hint: E = Pt and E = QV. Same energy — different ways to calculate it.

Exam Q: "Calculate the energy transferred by a 60 W bulb in 5 minutes." [2 marks]
→ E = Pt = 60 × (5 × 60) = 60 × 300 = **18,000 J**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p2-equations

## SECTION 2.3 — Series and Parallel Circuits

---

**P2-08** | Topic: Series Circuits | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about current, voltage and resistance in **series** circuits.

A:
- **Current** is the **same** at every point in the circuit
- **Voltage** is **shared** between components (voltages add up to the supply)
- **Total resistance** is the **sum** of all individual resistances: Rₜ = R₁ + R₂

Hint: Series = one path. Same current. Shared voltage. Resistance adds up.

Exam Q: "In a series circuit, two resistors of 3 Ω and 5 Ω are connected. What is the total resistance?" [1 mark]
→ Rₜ = 3 + 5 = **8 Ω**.

Notes link: /notes/p2-circuits

---

**P2-09** | Topic: Parallel Circuits | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about current, voltage and resistance in **parallel** circuits.

A:
- **Voltage** is the **same** across each branch
- **Current** is **shared** — total current = sum of branch currents
- **Total resistance** is **less** than the smallest individual resistor

Hint: Parallel = multiple paths. Same voltage. Shared current. Resistance decreases.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage of connecting components in parallel rather than series." [1 mark]
→ Any 1 from: same voltage across each component / if one fails others keep working / individual switches possible.

Notes link: /notes/p2-circuits

---

**P2-10** | Topic: Circuits | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 differences** between series and parallel circuits.

A:
- **Series**: one path for current / components share the voltage / total resistance increases with more components
- **Parallel**: multiple paths for current / same voltage across each branch / total resistance decreases with more components

Hint: Series = one loop. Parallel = multiple loops. Home wiring uses parallel.

Exam Q: "Give two differences between series and parallel circuits." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 comparative differences from above = 2 marks.

Notes link: /notes/p2-circuits

## SECTION 2.4 — I-V Graphs (IV Characteristics)

---

**P2-11** | Topic: IV Graphs | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **I-V graph** for a **fixed resistor** (ohmic conductor).

A:
- Current and potential difference are **directly proportional**
- The graph is a **straight line** through the origin
- **Resistance is constant** — doesn't change with temperature

Hint: Straight line through origin = ohmic conductor = constant resistance.

Exam Q: "Describe the I-V graph for a fixed resistor." [2 marks]
→ Straight line through origin (1) + current directly proportional to potential difference (1).

Notes link: /notes/p2-iv-graphs

---

**P2-12** | Topic: IV Graphs | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **I-V graph** for a **filament lamp**.

A:
- The graph is a **curve** — not a straight line
- Resistance **increases** as temperature increases
- As current increases, ions in the filament **vibrate more** — more collisions with electrons — greater resistance

Hint: Curved line = resistance not constant. Gets hotter = higher resistance = less current for same voltage.

Exam Q: "Describe the I-V graph for a filament lamp and explain the shape." [3 marks]
→ Curved line / not straight (1) + resistance increases with temperature (1) + increased ion vibration causes more collisions with electrons (1).

Notes link: /notes/p2-iv-graphs

---

**P2-13** | Topic: IV Graphs | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about the **I-V graph** for a **diode**.

A:
- Current only flows in **one direction** — very high resistance in the reverse direction
- The graph shows current only on **one side** of the origin

Hint: Diode = one-way valve for current. Forward = current flows. Reverse = almost no current.

Exam Q: "Describe how a diode behaves in an electric circuit." [2 marks]
→ Current flows in one direction only (1) + very high resistance in reverse direction (1).

Notes link: /notes/p2-iv-graphs

## SECTION 2.5 — Mains Electricity

---

**P2-14** | Topic: Mains Electricity | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **mains electricity** in the UK.

A:
- **Alternating current (AC)** — current regularly changes direction
- Voltage of **230 V**
- Frequency of **50 Hz**

Hint: UK mains = AC, 230 V, 50 Hz. Batteries give DC.

Exam Q: "State the voltage and frequency of UK mains electricity." [2 marks]
→ 230 V (1) + 50 Hz (1).

**P2-15** | Topic: Mains Electricity | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **alternating current (AC)** and **direct current (DC)**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **AC** — current regularly **changes direction** (e.g. mains electricity)
- **DC** — current flows in **one direction only** (e.g. batteries, cells)

Hint: AC = alternates direction. DC = direct, one way only.

Exam Q: "What is the difference between AC and DC?" [2 marks]
→ AC regularly changes direction (1) + DC flows in one direction only (1).

Notes link: /notes/p2-mains

---

**P2-16** | Topic: Mains Electricity | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **three-core cable**.

A:
- **Live wire (brown)** — carries the current to the appliance at 230 V
- **Neutral wire (blue)** — completes the circuit, returns current, at 0 V
- **Earth wire (green and yellow)** — safety wire, carries current to earth if live wire touches metal casing

Hint: Brown = live (dangerous). Blue = neutral (safe). Green/yellow = earth (safety).

Exam Q: "Describe the function of the earth wire in a three-core cable." [2 marks]
→ Carries current to earth (1) + if live wire touches the metal casing of the appliance (1).

## SECTION 2.6 — The National Grid

---

**P2-17** | Topic: National Grid | Tier: F

Q: What is the **National Grid**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A series of **cables and transformers** linking power stations to consumers
- Transmits electricity at **very high voltage** to reduce energy losses

Hint: The network that gets electricity from power stations to your home.

Exam Q: "What is the National Grid?" [2 marks]
→ A system of cables and transformers (1) + linking power stations to consumers (1).

Notes link: /notes/p2-national-grid

---

**P2-18** | Topic: National Grid | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **step-up and step-down transformers** in the National Grid.

A:
- **Step-up transformers** increase voltage for **transmission** — this reduces the current
- Lower current = **less energy lost** as heat in the cables — more efficient
- **Step-down transformers** reduce voltage back to **230 V** for safe use by consumers

Hint: Step up → high voltage, low current → less heat loss → efficient. Step down → safe for homes.

Exam Q: "Explain why electricity is transmitted at high voltage in the National Grid." [3 marks]
→ Step-up transformer increases voltage (1) + reduces current (1) + less energy lost as heat in cables (1).
