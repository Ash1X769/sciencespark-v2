## SECTION 1.1 — Atoms, Elements, Compounds and Mixtures

---

**C1-01** | Topic: Atoms | Tier: F

Q: What is an **atom**? *(1 fact)*

A:
- The **smallest part** of an element that can exist

Hint: Everything is made of atoms. They're tiny — about 0.1 nm across.

Exam Q: "What is an atom?" [1 mark]
→ Smallest part of an element that can exist.

**C1-02** | Topic: Atoms | Tier: F

Q: What is an **element**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A substance made of only **one type of atom**
- Cannot be broken down into **simpler substances** by chemical reactions

Hint: Gold, oxygen, carbon are elements. Pure substance, one type of atom only.

Exam Q: "What is an element?" [1 mark]
→ A substance made of only one type of atom.

Notes link: /notes/c1-atoms

---

**C1-03** | Topic: Atoms | Tier: F

Q: What is a **compound**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A substance made of **two or more elements**
- **Chemically bonded** together in fixed proportions

Hint: Water (H₂O) = hydrogen + oxygen chemically joined. Can only be separated by chemical reactions.

Exam Q: "What is a compound?" [2 marks]
→ Two or more elements (1) + chemically bonded together (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-atoms

---

**C1-04** | Topic: Atoms | Tier: F

Q: What is a **mixture**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Two or more substances **not chemically bonded**
- The chemical properties of each substance are **unchanged**
- Can be separated by **physical methods**

Hint: Air is a mixture — nitrogen and oxygen not bonded, can be separated.

Exam Q: "What is the difference between a compound and a mixture?" [2 marks]
→ Compound = chemically bonded / can only be separated by chemical reactions (1). Mixture = not chemically bonded / can be separated by physical methods (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-atoms

---

**C1-05** | Topic: Atoms | Tier: F

Q: Give **5 physical methods** used to **separate mixtures**. *(5 facts)*

A:
- **Filtration** — separates an insoluble solid from a liquid
- **Crystallisation** — separates a dissolved solid from a solution
- **Simple distillation** — separates a solvent from a solution
- **Fractional distillation** — separates liquids with different boiling points
- **Chromatography** — separates dissolved substances in a mixture

Hint: Filter, Crystallise, Distil, Fractionate, Chromatograph — all physical, no new substances made.

Exam Q: "Describe how you would separate salt from a salt solution." [2 marks]
→ Evaporate / crystallise the solution (1) + heat to remove water / leave to crystallise (1).

## SECTION 1.2 — The Structure of an Atom

---

**C1-06** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: What are the **3 subatomic particles**, their **charges** and **relative masses**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Proton** — charge **+1** — relative mass **1**
- **Neutron** — charge **0** — relative mass **1**
- **Electron** — charge **−1** — relative mass very small (negligible)

Hint: Protons and Neutrons in the Nucleus. Electrons outside in shells.

Exam Q: "State the relative charge of a neutron." [1 mark] *(AQA June 2024)*
→ 0 / neutral.

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-structure

---

**C1-07** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: Where are the **subatomic particles** found in an atom? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Protons** and **neutrons** are found in the **nucleus** at the centre
- **Electrons** orbit the nucleus in **shells** (energy levels)

Hint: Nucleus = protons + neutrons. Shells = electrons orbiting outside.

Exam Q: "State where electrons are found in an atom." [1 mark]
→ In shells / energy levels / orbiting the nucleus.

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-structure

---

**C1-08** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **size of atoms**.

A:
- Atoms have a radius of about **0.1 nm** (1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m)
- The radius of a nucleus is less than **1/10 000** of the atom
- Almost all of the mass of an atom is in the **nucleus**

Hint: Incredibly small. The nucleus is even tinier — most of the atom is empty space.

Exam Q: "What is the approximate radius of an atom?" [1 mark] *(AQA June 2024)*
→ 0.1 nm / 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-structure

---

**C1-09** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: What is the **atomic number** of an element? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The number of **protons** in the nucleus
- In a neutral atom, also equals the number of **electrons**

Hint: Atomic number = protons. Found at the bottom of the element symbol on the periodic table.

Exam Q: "What is the atomic number of an element?" [1 mark] *(AQA June 2024)*
→ The number of protons in the nucleus.

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-structure

---

**C1-10** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: What is the **mass number** of an atom? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **total number of protons and neutrons** in the nucleus
- Number of neutrons = **mass number − atomic number**

Hint: Mass number = protons + neutrons. Atomic number = protons only.

Exam Q: "An atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23. How many neutrons does it have?" [1 mark]
→ 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-structure

**C1-11** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: What are **isotopes**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Atoms of the **same element**
- With the **same number of protons**
- But **different numbers of neutrons**

Hint: Same element, different mass. Carbon-12 and Carbon-14 are isotopes.

Exam Q: "What are isotopes?" [2 marks]
→ Same number of protons (1) + different number of neutrons (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-structure

---

**C1-12** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: H

Q: What is the **relative atomic mass (Ar)**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **average mass** of all atoms of an element
- Takes into account the **relative abundance** of each isotope

Hint: Ar considers all isotopes and how common each one is. Weighted average.

Exam Q: "Chlorine has two isotopes: Cl-35 (75%) and Cl-37 (25%). Calculate the Ar." [2 marks]
→ (35 × 75 + 37 × 25) ÷ 100 = 35.5. Show working for both marks.

## SECTION 1.3 — Development of the Model of the Atom

---

**C1-13** | Topic: Atomic Models | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** in the **development of the model of the atom**.

A:
- **Before electrons**: atoms thought to be tiny, indivisible spheres
- **Thomson**: discovered the electron → proposed the **plum pudding model** — ball of positive charge with negative electrons embedded in it
- **Rutherford**: alpha particle scattering experiment showed mass is concentrated in a tiny, positively charged **nucleus** → replaced plum pudding model
- **Bohr**: suggested electrons orbit the nucleus at **specific distances** (shells) — calculations matched experimental data

Hint: Sphere → Plum pudding → Nuclear model → Bohr shells

Exam Q: "Describe how the model of the atom changed from the plum pudding model to the nuclear model." [3 marks]
→ Alpha particles fired at gold foil (1) + most passed through / some deflected back (1) + showed mass concentrated in a small positive nucleus (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-models

---

**C1-14** | Topic: Atomic Models | Tier: F

Q: What was the **plum pudding model** of the atom? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The atom was thought to be a **ball of positive charge**
- With **negative electrons** embedded in it (like plums in a pudding)

Hint: Plum pudding = positive ball + electrons dotted through it. Before Rutherford.

Exam Q: "Describe the plum pudding model of the atom." [2 marks]
→ Ball of positive charge (1) + negative electrons embedded throughout (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-models

---

**C1-15** | Topic: Atomic Models | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **alpha particle scattering experiment**.

A:
- Alpha particles were fired at a **thin gold foil**
- Most passed **straight through** — atom is mostly empty space
- A small number were **deflected back** — showed a tiny, dense, positive nucleus exists

Hint: Most go through (empty space). Some bounce back (nucleus). Changed the model.

Exam Q: "Describe the results of the alpha particle scattering experiment and what they showed." [3 marks]
→ Most particles passed through (1) + some deflected at large angles (1) + showed mass concentrated in a small positive nucleus (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-models

---

**C1-16** | Topic: Atomic Models | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about **Chadwick's** contribution to the atomic model.

A:
- Provided experimental evidence for the existence of **neutrons** within the nucleus
- This was about **20 years** after the nucleus became an accepted idea

Hint: Chadwick = neutrons. Last piece of the puzzle.

Exam Q: "What did Chadwick's experimental work show?" [1 mark]
→ Provided evidence for the existence of neutrons in the nucleus.

Notes link: /notes/c1-atomic-models

## SECTION 1.4 — Electronic Structure

---

**C1-17** | Topic: Electronic Structure | Tier: F

Q: What are the **rules** for filling electron shells? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Shell 1 holds a maximum of **2** electrons
- Shell 2 holds a maximum of **8** electrons
- Shell 3 holds a maximum of **8** electrons (for first 20 elements)

Hint: 2, 8, 8. Always fill from the inside out.

Exam Q: "How many electrons are in the outer shell of a sodium atom (atomic number 11)?" [1 mark]
→ Sodium = 2,8,1. Outer shell = 1 electron.

Notes link: /notes/c1-electronic-structure

---

**C1-18** | Topic: Electronic Structure | Tier: F

Q: Write the **electronic structures** of these elements. *(4 facts)*

A:
- **Sodium** (11): **2,8,1**
- **Magnesium** (12): **2,8,2**
- **Chlorine** (17): **2,8,7**
- **Calcium** (20): **2,8,8,2**

Hint: Count the protons → fill shells 2,8,8 from inside out.

Exam Q: "Write the electronic structure of a sodium atom." [1 mark]
→ 2,8,1.

## SECTION 1.5 — The Periodic Table

---

**C1-21** | Topic: Periodic Table | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how the **periodic table is arranged**.

A:
- Elements are arranged in order of **atomic number**
- Elements in the same **group** have similar **chemical properties**
- Elements in the same **period** have the same number of **electron shells**

Hint: Atomic number order. Group = similar properties (same outer electrons). Period = same shells.

Exam Q: "How are elements arranged in the periodic table?" [2 marks]
→ In order of atomic number (1) + elements in same group have similar properties (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-periodic-table

---

**C1-22** | Topic: Periodic Table | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **development of the periodic table**.

A:
- Early scientists arranged elements in order of **atomic weights** — some were placed in wrong groups
- **Mendeleev** left **gaps** for undiscovered elements and changed the order in some places
- When new elements were discovered, they **matched Mendeleev's predictions** — the table was accepted

Hint: Atomic weight order had problems → Mendeleev left gaps → gaps were filled correctly → accepted.

Exam Q: "Describe how Mendeleev's periodic table was different from earlier attempts." [2 marks]
→ Left gaps for undiscovered elements (1) + changed order in places where atomic weight was misleading (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-periodic-table

---

**C1-23** | Topic: Periodic Table | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **metals vs non-metals** in the periodic table.

A:
- Elements that form **positive ions** are **metals**
- Elements that **don't** form positive ions are **non-metals**
- Metals are found on the **left and bottom** — non-metals on the **right and top**

Hint: Most elements are metals. Non-metals are top-right of the table.

Exam Q: "Where are metals found in the periodic table?" [1 mark]
→ Left side and bottom / towards the left.

Notes link: /notes/c1-periodic-table

## SECTION 1.6 — Group 1 — Alkali Metals

---

**C1-24** | Topic: Group 1 | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **alkali metals** (Group 1).

A:
- All have **1 electron** in their outer shell
- They are **soft** metals with **low melting points**
- They are very **reactive**

Hint: 1 outer electron = very reactive. Soft enough to cut with a knife.

Exam Q: "What name is given to Group 1 elements?" [1 mark] *(AQA June 2024)*
→ Alkali metals.

Notes link: /notes/c1-group1

---

**C1-25** | Topic: Group 1 | Tier: F

Q: What are the **products** when an alkali metal reacts with **water**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **metal hydroxide** (e.g. lithium hydroxide, sodium hydroxide)
- **Hydrogen** gas

Hint: Metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen. Always these two products.

Exam Q: "What are the products when sodium reacts with water?" [2 marks]
→ Sodium hydroxide (1) + hydrogen (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-group1

---

**C1-26** | Topic: Group 1 | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **reactivity trend** going down Group 1.

A:
- Reactivity **increases** going down the group
- The outer electron is in a **higher energy shell** — further from the nucleus
- The electron is **lost more easily** — so the metal reacts faster

Hint: Further from nucleus = easier to lose = more reactive. Li < Na < K < Rb < Cs.

Exam Q: "Describe why potassium is more reactive than lithium." [3 marks]
→ Outer electron in higher shell / further from nucleus (1) + weaker attraction between nucleus and outer electron (1) + electron lost more easily (1).

Notes link: /notes/c1-group1

**C1-27** | Topic: Group 1 | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 trends** in **physical properties** going down Group 1.

A:
- **Melting points decrease** going down the group
- **Boiling points decrease** going down the group
- **Density increases** going down the group

Hint: Down Group 1 = lower melting/boiling points, higher density. Opposite to what you might expect!

Exam Q: "Describe the trend in melting point going down Group 1." [1 mark]
→ Melting point decreases going down the group.

## SECTION 1.7 — Group 7 — Halogens

---

**C1-28** | Topic: Group 7 | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **halogens** (Group 7).

A:
- All have **7 electrons** in their outer shell
- They exist as **diatomic molecules** (pairs of atoms — e.g. Cl₂, Br₂, I₂)
- They are **non-metals** and very **reactive**

Hint: 7 outer electrons = need 1 more = reactive. Always in pairs.

Exam Q: "Give the formula of a chlorine molecule." [1 mark]
→ Cl₂.

Notes link: /notes/c1-group7

- **Chlorine (Cl₂)** — green/yellow gas
- **Bromine (Br₂)** — orange/brown liquid
- **Iodine (I₂)** — grey/black solid

→ Orange/brown liquid.

**C1-30** | Topic: Group 7 | Tier: F

A:
- **Melting points increase** going down the group
- **Boiling points increase** going down the group
- Colour becomes **darker** going down the group

Hint: Down Group 7 = higher melting/boiling points, darker colour. Opposite to Group 1.

Exam Q: "Describe the trend in boiling point going down Group 7." [1 mark]

**C1-31** | Topic: Group 7 | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **reactivity trend** going down Group 7.

A:
- Reactivity **decreases** going down the group
- The outer shell is **further from the nucleus** going down
- It's **harder to attract** an extra electron to fill the outer shell

Hint: Further from nucleus = harder to gain electron = less reactive. F > Cl > Br > I.

Exam Q: "Describe why fluorine is more reactive than chlorine." [3 marks]
→ Fewer electron shells / outer shell closer to nucleus (1) + stronger attraction for incoming electron (1) + gains electron more easily (1).

**C1-32** | Topic: Group 7 | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about **displacement reactions** of halogens.

A:
- A **more reactive** halogen displaces a **less reactive** halogen from a salt solution
- e.g. Chlorine + potassium bromide → potassium chloride + bromine (solution turns orange)

Hint: More reactive kicks out the less reactive one. Cl > Br > I in reactivity.

Exam Q: "Chlorine is added to potassium iodide solution. What do you observe?" [2 marks]

Q: Give **2 facts** about why **chlorine is more reactive** than bromine.

A:
- Chlorine has **fewer electron shells** than bromine
- The outer shell is **closer to the nucleus** — stronger attraction — gains an electron **more easily**

Hint: Fewer shells = closer nucleus = stronger pull on incoming electron = more reactive.

Exam Q: "Describe why chlorine is more reactive than bromine." [2 marks] *(AQA June 2024, Q07)*
→ Fewer shells / outer shell closer to nucleus (1) + stronger attraction for electron / gains electron more easily (1).

## SECTION 1.8 — Group 0 — Noble Gases

---

**C1-34** | Topic: Group 0 | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **noble gases** (Group 0).

A:
- Have a **full outer shell** of electrons
- Are **very unreactive** — don't easily form molecules
- Exist as **individual atoms** — not molecules

Hint: Full outer shell = stable = won't react. He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe.

Exam Q: "Why are noble gases unreactive?" [1 mark]
→ They have a full outer shell of electrons / stable electron arrangement.

Notes link: /notes/c1-group0

---

**C1-35** | Topic: Group 0 | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about the **boiling point trend** going down Group 0.

A:
- Boiling points **increase** going down the group
- Because atoms get **larger** — stronger forces between atoms — more energy needed to boil

Hint: Bigger atom = stronger intermolecular forces = higher boiling point. He boils at −269°C, Xe at −108°C.

Exam Q: "Describe the trend in boiling points of the noble gases going down Group 0." [2 marks]
→ Boiling point increases going down the group (1) + atoms get larger / stronger forces between them (1).
