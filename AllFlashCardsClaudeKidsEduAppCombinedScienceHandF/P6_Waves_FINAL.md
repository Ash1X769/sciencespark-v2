## SECTION 6.1 — Wave Properties

---

**P6-01** | Topic: Wave Properties | Tier: F

Q: Give the **5 key wave properties** and their definitions.

A:
- **Amplitude** — maximum displacement from the undisturbed position
- **Wavelength** — distance from one point on a wave to the equivalent point on the next wave
- **Frequency** — number of waves passing a point per second (Hz)
- **Period** — time for one complete wave to pass a point (s)
- **Wave speed** — distance the wave travels per second (m/s)

Hint: Amplitude (height), Wavelength (length), Frequency (per second), Period (time for one), Speed (m/s).

Exam Q: "What is meant by the amplitude of a wave?" [1 mark]
→ Maximum displacement from the undisturbed / rest position.

**P6-02** | Topic: Wave Properties | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **transverse** and **longitudinal** waves? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Transverse** — oscillations are **perpendicular (at right angles)** to the direction of travel (e.g. light, all EM waves, water waves)
- **Longitudinal** — oscillations are **parallel** to the direction of travel (e.g. sound waves)

Hint: Transverse = across. Longitudinal = along. Sound is longitudinal, light is transverse.

Exam Q: "What is the difference between transverse and longitudinal waves?" [2 marks]
→ Transverse: oscillations perpendicular to direction of travel (1). Longitudinal: oscillations parallel to direction of travel (1).

**P6-03** | Topic: Wave Properties | Tier: F

Q: What are **compressions** and **rarefactions** in a longitudinal wave? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Compressions** — regions where particles are **closest together** (high pressure)
- **Rarefactions** — regions where particles are **furthest apart** (low pressure)

Hint: Compression = squashed together. Rarefaction = spread out. Both found in sound waves.

Exam Q: "Describe a compression in a longitudinal wave." [1 mark]
→ Region where particles are closest together / highest pressure.

**P6-04** | Topic: Wave Properties | Tier: F

Q: What is the **wave equation**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Wave speed = frequency × wavelength**
- **v = fλ**
- Units: speed in **m/s**, frequency in **Hz**, wavelength in **m**

Hint: v = fλ. Speed equals frequency times wavelength.

Exam Q: "Calculate the speed of a wave with a frequency of 50 Hz and wavelength of 3 m." [2 marks]
→ v = 50 × 3 = **150 m/s**. Show working.

**P6-05** | Topic: Wave Properties | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **reflection** of waves.

A:
- Waves **bounce off** a surface
- The **angle of incidence** equals the **angle of reflection**
- Both angles measured from the **normal** (perpendicular to the surface)

Hint: Angle in = angle out. Always measured from the normal, not the surface.

Exam Q: "State the law of reflection." [1 mark]
→ Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.

Notes link: /notes/p6-reflection

**P6-06** | Topic: Wave Properties | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **refraction** of waves.

A:
- Waves **change direction** when they enter a material of **different density**
- This happens because the wave **changes speed** in the new material
- Towards the normal if slowing down; away from the normal if speeding up

Hint: Refraction = direction change due to speed change at a boundary.

Exam Q: "What causes refraction of waves?" [2 marks]
→ Change in speed (1) + when wave enters a material of different density (1).

**P6-08** | Topic: EM Spectrum | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about **all electromagnetic waves**.

A:
- All are **transverse** waves
- All travel at **3 × 10⁸ m/s** in a vacuum (speed of light)
- They transfer **energy** from source to absorber
- They can travel through a **vacuum** (empty space)

Hint: All EM waves: transverse, travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, can travel through space.

Exam Q: "Give two properties shared by all electromagnetic waves." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: transverse / travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s / transfer energy / travel through vacuum.

## SECTION 6.3 — Sound Waves

---

**P6-15** | Topic: Sound Waves | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 differences** between **electromagnetic waves** and **sound waves**.

A:
- EM waves travel at **3 × 10⁸ m/s** — sound travels at about **330 m/s** in air (much slower)
- EM waves can travel through a **vacuum** — sound **cannot**
- EM waves are **transverse** — sound is **longitudinal**

Hint: EM = faster, vacuum, transverse. Sound = slower, needs a medium, longitudinal.

Exam Q: "Give two differences between electromagnetic waves and sound waves." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 comparative differences from above = 2 marks.

**P6-16** | Topic: Sound Waves | Tier: F

Q: Give **5 steps** for **measuring the speed of sound** using an echo.

A:
- Measure the **distance** to a wall or building
- Fire a **starting pistol** (or clap) and **start a timer**
- **Stop the timer** when the echo is heard
- **Halve** the time (sound travels to wall and back)
- Calculate speed = **distance ÷ half-time**

Hint: Distance measured. Fire → echo → timer stopped. Halve the time (goes there and back).

Exam Q: "Describe how to measure the speed of sound using an echo." [4 marks]
→ Measure distance to wall (1) + make loud sound and start timer (1) + stop timer when echo heard (1) + halve time / calculate speed = distance ÷ time (1).
