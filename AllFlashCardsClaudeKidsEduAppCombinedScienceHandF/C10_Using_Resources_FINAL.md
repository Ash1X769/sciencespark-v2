## SECTION 10.1 — Finite and Renewable Resources

---

**C10-01** | Topic: Resources | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **finite** and **renewable** resources? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Finite** (non-renewable) resources are used up **faster** than they form — will eventually run out
- **Renewable** resources are replaced as quickly as they are used — won't run out

Hint: Finite = runs out (coal, oil). Renewable = replaced (wood, wind, solar).

Exam Q: "What is meant by a finite resource?" [1 mark]
→ A resource being used up faster than it is formed / will eventually run out.

**C10-02** | Topic: Resources | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about **sustainable development**.

A:
- Meeting the needs of the **current population**
- Without stopping **future generations** from meeting their own needs

Hint: Using resources wisely so there's enough for people in the future too.

Exam Q: "What is meant by sustainable development?" [2 marks]
→ Meeting needs of current population (1) + without preventing future generations meeting their needs (1).

## SECTION 10.2 — Water Treatment

---

**C10-03** | Topic: Water | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **potable**, **fresh** and **pure** water? *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Potable** water — safe to **drink** — has low levels of dissolved salts and microbes
- **Fresh** water — has **low levels of dissolved salts** — but may still contain microbes
- **Pure** water — contains **only water molecules** — nothing else dissolved

Hint: Potable = safe to drink. Fresh = low salt. Pure = only H₂O. Tap water is potable but not pure.

Exam Q: "What is meant by potable water?" [1 mark]
→ Water that is safe to drink / has acceptable levels of dissolved substances and microbes.

Notes link: /notes/c10-water

---

**C10-04** | Topic: Water | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 steps** for treating **fresh water** to make it **potable** (in the UK).

A:
- Pass through **filter beds** to remove particles and larger organisms
- **Sedimentation** — allow suspended particles to settle
- **Sterilise** to kill microbes — using chlorine, ozone or ultraviolet light

Hint: Filter → settle → sterilise. Three steps to safe drinking water.

Exam Q: "Describe how fresh water is treated to make it potable." [3 marks]
→ Filtration to remove particles (1) + sedimentation (1) + sterilisation / chlorination (1).

Notes link: /notes/c10-water

---

**C10-05** | Topic: Water | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 methods** of producing potable water from **sea water** (desalination).

A:
- **Distillation** — water heated to 100°C, evaporates, leaving salt behind, then condensed
- **Reverse osmosis** — pressure applied so water molecules pass through a partially permeable membrane, leaving salt behind

Hint: Distil (heat + condense) or reverse osmosis (pressure + membrane). Both expensive — need lots of energy.

Exam Q: "Describe how desalination produces potable water from seawater." [2 marks]
→ Any valid method (distillation / reverse osmosis) with correct description = 2 marks.

Notes link: /notes/c10-water

---

**C10-06** | Topic: Water | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about the **disadvantage** of desalination.

A:
- Requires a **very large amount of energy**
- This makes it **expensive** — only used where fresh water is very scarce

Hint: Energy intensive = costly. Used in places like the Middle East where fresh water is limited.

Exam Q: "Give one disadvantage of desalination as a way of producing potable water." [1 mark]
→ Requires a lot of energy / expensive.

Notes link: /notes/c10-water

## SECTION 10.3 — Sewage Treatment

---

**C10-07** | Topic: Sewage Treatment | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** of **sewage treatment**.

A:
- **Screening** — removes large solids (rags, plastics) that could block pipes
- **Sedimentation** — suspended particles settle to form **sewage sludge**
- **Anaerobic digestion** of sewage sludge — bacteria digest sludge without oxygen, producing methane and CO₂
- **Aerobic biological treatment** — aerobic bacteria digest remaining organic matter in the effluent

Hint: Screen → settle → digest anaerobically → treat aerobically → safe to release.

Exam Q: "Describe how sewage is treated before water is released back into the environment." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

## SECTION 10.4 — Alternative Methods of Metal Extraction

---

**C10-08** | Topic: Metal Extraction | Tier: H

Q: What is **bioleaching**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Bacteria grow on **low-grade copper ores**
- They produce a **leachate** (liquid) that contains **copper compounds**

Hint: Bacteria + low-grade ore → copper-containing liquid → extract copper from liquid.

Exam Q: "Describe bioleaching as a method of extracting copper." [2 marks]
→ Bacteria grow on low-grade ore (1) + produce a leachate containing copper compounds (1).

Notes link: /notes/c10-metal-extraction

---

**C10-09** | Topic: Metal Extraction | Tier: H

Q: What is **phytomining**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Plants** are grown on **low-grade copper ores**
- The plants **absorb copper** through their roots
- The plants are **burned** — the ash contains copper compounds which are then extracted

Hint: Plants absorb copper → burn plants → copper compounds in ash → extract copper.

Exam Q: "Describe phytomining as a method of extracting copper." [3 marks]
→ Plants grown on low-grade ore (1) + plants absorb copper (1) + plants burned / copper compounds in ash (1).

Notes link: /notes/c10-metal-extraction

---

**C10-10** | Topic: Metal Extraction | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 methods** for obtaining **pure copper** from the copper compounds produced in bioleaching or phytomining.

A:
- **Displacement using scrap iron** — iron displaces copper as iron is more reactive — cheap
- **Electrolysis** — copper ions in solution attracted to the cathode — produces very pure copper

Hint: Iron displaces copper (cheap). Electrolysis gives purer copper (more expensive).

Exam Q: "Give one advantage of using iron displacement rather than electrolysis to obtain copper." [1 mark]
→ Cheaper / scrap iron is inexpensive / doesn't need electricity.

Notes link: /notes/c10-metal-extraction

---

**C10-11** | Topic: Metal Extraction | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 advantages** of bioleaching and phytomining over traditional mining.

A:
- Can extract copper from **low-grade ores** that wouldn't be worth mining traditionally
- Less environmental damage than traditional **open-cast mining**

Hint: Low-grade ores usable + less damaging = good alternatives.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage of phytomining over traditional copper mining." [1 mark]
→ Any 1 from: uses low-grade ores / less environmental damage / no large-scale excavation.

Notes link: /notes/c10-metal-extraction

## SECTION 10.5 — Life Cycle Assessment and Recycling

---

**C10-12** | Topic: LCA | Tier: F

Q: What is a **life cycle assessment (LCA)**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Assesses the **environmental impact** of a product
- Covers every stage from **raw materials** to **disposal**

Hint: Cradle to grave — tracks environmental impact throughout a product's entire life.

Exam Q: "What is a life cycle assessment?" [2 marks]
→ Assesses environmental impact (1) + at every stage of a product's life (1).

Notes link: /notes/c10-lca

---

**C10-13** | Topic: LCA | Tier: F

Q: Give the **4 stages** of a **life cycle assessment**.

A:
- Extraction and processing of **raw materials**
- **Manufacturing** and packaging
- **Use** and operation during its lifetime
- **Disposal** at the end of its useful life

Hint: Raw materials → Manufacture → Use → Disposal. All four stages assessed.

Exam Q: "List the four stages covered in a life cycle assessment." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

Notes link: /notes/c10-lca

**C10-14** | Topic: LCA | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about **limitations** of life cycle assessments.

A:
- Some aspects involve **judgements** — not all environmental impacts can be measured numerically
- LCAs can be **biased** — a company may manipulate data to make their product appear more sustainable

Hint: Not always objective. Difficult to assign numbers to everything. Can be used selectively.

Exam Q: "Give one limitation of a life cycle assessment." [1 mark]
→ Involves subjective judgements / can be manipulated / not all impacts easily quantifiable.

Q: Give **3 benefits** of **recycling** materials.

A:
- Conserves **finite resources** — less need to extract raw materials
- Requires **less energy** than extracting and processing new materials
- Reduces waste going to **landfill**

Hint: Saves resources, saves energy, reduces waste. Three reasons to recycle.

Q: Give **2 facts** about **reuse** of products.

A:

- Only suitable for some products — e.g. **glass bottles**, **bags for life**

## SECTION 10.6 — Rusting and Alloys

---

**C10-17** | Topic: Rusting | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **rusting** of iron.

A:
- Iron reacts with both **oxygen** and **water** to form rust
- Both oxygen AND water are **needed** — neither alone causes rusting
- Rust = **hydrated iron(III) oxide**

Hint: Iron + oxygen + water → rust. Remove either one and rusting stops.

Exam Q: "What two substances must be present for iron to rust?" [2 marks]
→ Oxygen (1) + water (1).

Notes link: /notes/c10-rusting

---

**C10-18** | Topic: Rusting | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 methods** of **preventing rusting**.

A:
- **Painting** or coating with oil/grease — forms a **barrier** preventing contact with oxygen and water
- **Galvanising** — coating with zinc — provides barrier AND **sacrificial protection**
- Using **stainless steel** — an alloy of iron, chromium and nickel that doesn't corrode

Hint: Paint (barrier), galvanise (barrier + sacrificial), stainless steel (alloy that resists rust).

Exam Q: "Give two ways of preventing iron from rusting." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: painting / oiling / galvanising / electroplating / alloying / stainless steel.

Notes link: /notes/c10-rusting

---

**C10-19** | Topic: Rusting | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about **sacrificial protection**.

A:
- A **more reactive metal** (e.g. zinc or magnesium) is attached to the iron
- The more reactive metal **oxidises preferentially** — corrodes instead of the iron
- The iron is **protected** even if the coating is scratched

Hint: Zinc sacrifices itself so the iron doesn't rust. Even scratched, the zinc corrodes first.

Exam Q: "Describe how sacrificial protection prevents rusting of iron." [3 marks]
→ More reactive metal attached (1) + oxidises / corrodes preferentially (1) + iron protected even if scratched (1).

Notes link: /notes/c10-rusting
