## SECTION 1.1 — Energy Stores

---

**P1-01** | Topic: Energy Stores | Tier: F

Q: List the **8 energy stores**. *(8 facts)*

A:
- **Chemical** — energy stored in chemicals waiting to react (e.g. food, fuel, batteries)
- **Kinetic** — energy stored in moving objects
- **Gravitational potential** — energy stored in objects raised against gravity
- **Elastic potential** — energy stored in stretched or compressed objects
- **Internal (thermal)** — energy stored in the movement of particles
- **Nuclear** — energy stored in atomic nuclei
- **Magnetic** — energy stored in magnets attracting or repelling
- **Electrostatic** — energy stored in electric charges attracting or repelling

Hint: Chemical, Kinetic, Gravitational, Elastic, Internal, Nuclear, Magnetic, Electrostatic — CKGEINME

Exam Q: "Give two examples of energy stores." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from the list = 2 marks.

**P1-02** | Topic: Energy Stores | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 methods** of **energy transfer**.

A:
- **Mechanical** — a force moves through a distance (e.g. pushing, pulling)
- **Electrical** — charge moves through a circuit
- **Radiation** — energy carried by electromagnetic waves (e.g. light, infrared)
- **Heating** — energy transferred from a hotter to a cooler object

Hint: Mechanical, Electrical, Radiation, Heating — MERH

Exam Q: "Give two methods by which energy can be transferred." [2 marks]

## SECTION 1.2 — Conservation of Energy

---

**P1-03** | Topic: Conservation of Energy | Tier: F

Q: What is the **law of conservation of energy**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Energy **cannot** be created or destroyed
- It can only be **transferred** from one store to another

Hint: Total energy always stays the same. It just moves between stores.

Exam Q: "State the law of conservation of energy." [1 mark]
→ Energy cannot be created or destroyed / only transferred between stores.

Notes link: /notes/p1-conservation

---

**P1-04** | Topic: Conservation of Energy | Tier: F

Q: What is **dissipation** of energy? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Energy that is **spread out** into the surroundings rather than transferred usefully
- Usually transferred as **heat** — this is "wasted" energy

Hint: Dissipated = wasted. Spreads out and becomes less useful.

Exam Q: "What is meant by dissipation of energy?" [2 marks]
→ Energy spread out to surroundings (1) + not usefully transferred / wasted as heat (1).

Notes link: /notes/p1-conservation

---

**P1-05** | Topic: Conservation of Energy | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 ways** of **reducing unwanted energy transfers**.

A:
- **Lubrication** — applying oil or grease reduces friction between surfaces — less heat wasted
- **Insulation** — using insulating materials reduces heat loss from buildings

Hint: Lubricate moving parts. Insulate hot things. Both reduce wasted energy.

Exam Q: "Give two ways of reducing unwanted energy transfers in machines." [2 marks]
→ Lubrication / insulation / streamlining / using more efficient components.

## SECTION 1.3 — Key Equations

---

**P1-06** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation for **kinetic energy**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Kinetic energy = 0.5 × mass × velocity²**
- **Eₖ = ½mv²**
- Units: energy in **J**, mass in **kg**, velocity in **m/s**

Hint: KE = half times mass times velocity squared. Speed squared makes a big difference.

Exam Q: "Calculate the kinetic energy of a 2 kg object moving at 3 m/s." [2 marks]
→ Eₖ = 0.5 × 2 × 3² = 0.5 × 2 × 9 = **9 J**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p1-equations

---

**P1-07** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation for **gravitational potential energy (GPE)**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **GPE = mass × gravitational field strength × height**
- **Eₚ = mgh**
- Units: energy in **J**, mass in **kg**, g in **N/kg** (10 N/kg on Earth), height in **m**

Hint: GPE = mgh. Mass times g (10) times height.

Exam Q: "Calculate the GPE of a 5 kg object raised 3 m above the ground. [g = 10 N/kg]" [2 marks]
→ Eₚ = 5 × 10 × 3 = **150 J**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p1-equations

---

**P1-08** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation for **power**? Give **both** versions. *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Power = energy transferred ÷ time** → **P = E/t**
- **Power = work done ÷ time** → **P = W/t**
- Units: power in **W**, energy/work in **J**, time in **s**

Hint: Power = how fast energy is transferred. P = E/t.

Exam Q: "A motor transfers 600 J of energy in 30 s. Calculate its power." [2 marks]
→ P = 600 ÷ 30 = **20 W**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p1-equations

---

**P1-09** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation for **efficiency**? Give **both** versions. *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input**
- **Efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input**
- Expressed as a **decimal** (0 to 1) or **percentage** (multiply by 100)

Hint: Efficiency = useful out ÷ total in. Can never exceed 1 (or 100%).

Exam Q: "A lamp transfers 40 J of useful light energy from 200 J of electrical energy. Calculate its efficiency." [2 marks]
→ Efficiency = 40 ÷ 200 = **0.2** (or 20%). Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p1-equations

---

**P1-10** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is **specific heat capacity (SHC)**? Give the equation. *(3 facts)*

A:
- The energy needed to raise **1 kg** of a material by **1°C**
- **Energy = mass × SHC × temperature change**
- **E = mcΔθ**
- Units: energy in **J**, mass in **kg**, SHC in **J/kg°C**, temperature change in **°C**

Hint: E = mcΔθ. More mass = more energy needed. Higher SHC = harder to heat up.

Exam Q: "Calculate the energy needed to heat 2 kg of water by 5°C. [SHC of water = 4200 J/kg°C]" [3 marks]
→ E = 2 × 4200 × 5 = **42,000 J**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p1-equations

## SECTION 1.4 — Energy Resources

---

**P1-11** | Topic: Energy Resources | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **fossil fuels** as an energy resource.

A:
- **Non-renewable** — will eventually run out
- **Reliable** — can generate electricity all the time
- Produces **CO₂** (causes global warming) and can produce **SO₂** (causes acid rain)

Hint: Reliable but polluting. Coal, oil and natural gas are fossil fuels.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage and one disadvantage of using fossil fuels to generate electricity." [2 marks]
→ Advantage: reliable / can generate all the time (1). Disadvantage: produces CO₂ / causes global warming (1).

Notes link: /notes/p1-energy-resources

---

**P1-12** | Topic: Energy Resources | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **nuclear fuel** as an energy resource.

A:
- **Non-renewable**
- **Reliable** — can generate electricity all the time
- Produces **no CO₂** during electricity generation — but creates **radioactive waste** that lasts thousands of years

Hint: Clean electricity but radioactive waste is a serious problem. Expensive to build and decommission.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage and one disadvantage of nuclear power." [2 marks]
→ Advantage: no CO₂ / reliable (1). Disadvantage: radioactive waste / expensive (1).

Notes link: /notes/p1-energy-resources

**P1-15** | Topic: Energy Resources | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **hydroelectric power** as an energy resource.

A:

- Produces **no CO₂** during electricity generation
- Can **block rivers** — stopping fish migration and damaging ecosystems. Also unreliable during droughts.

Hint: Clean but can harm river ecosystems. Needs a large dam.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage and one disadvantage of hydroelectric power." [2 marks]

- **Renewable**
- **Reliable** — can generate electricity all the time

**P1-18** | Topic: Energy Resources | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about **tidal** and **wave power** as energy resources.

A:
- Both **renewable** — produce **no CO₂** during generation
- Both **unreliable** — tides vary, wave power depends on sea conditions. Can damage coastal/tidal ecosystems.

Hint: Renewable and clean — but output varies. Tidal is more predictable than wave power.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage and one disadvantage of tidal power." [2 marks]

**P1-19** | Topic: Energy Resources | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **renewable** and **non-renewable** energy resources? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Renewable** resources will **not run out** — they are naturally replenished (e.g. wind, solar, tidal)
- **Non-renewable** resources will eventually **run out** — they are finite (e.g. fossil fuels, nuclear)

Hint: Renewable = won't run out. Non-renewable = will run out.

Exam Q: "What is meant by a renewable energy resource?" [1 mark]
→ One that will not run out / is naturally replenished.
