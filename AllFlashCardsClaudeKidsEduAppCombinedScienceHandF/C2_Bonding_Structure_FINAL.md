## SECTION 2.1 — The Three Types of Chemical Bond

---

**C2-01** | Topic: Chemical Bonds | Tier: F

Q: What are the **3 types** of chemical bond? *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Ionic** — between a metal and a non-metal — electrons are **transferred**
- **Covalent** — between non-metals — electrons are **shared**
- **Metallic** — in metals and alloys — electrons are **delocalised** (shared across all atoms)

Hint: Ionic = transfer. Covalent = sharing. Metallic = delocalised sharing.

Exam Q: "State the type of bonding in sodium chloride." [1 mark]
→ Ionic bonding.

## SECTION 2.2 — Ionic Bonding

---

**C2-02** | Topic: Ionic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **ionic bonding**.

A:
- Electrons are **transferred** from a metal atom to a non-metal atom
- The metal loses electrons → forms a **positive ion**
- The non-metal gains electrons → forms a **negative ion**

Hint: Metal GIVES electrons (positive). Non-metal TAKES electrons (negative).

Exam Q: "Describe ionic bonding." [3 marks]
→ Electron transfer (1) + metal forms positive ion (1) + non-metal forms negative ion (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-ionic-bonding

---

**C2-03** | Topic: Ionic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: What **charge** do ions have based on their **group number**? *(4 facts)*

A:
- **Group 1** metals → **+1** ion (e.g. Na⁺, K⁺)
- **Group 2** metals → **+2** ion (e.g. Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺)
- **Group 6** non-metals → **−2** ion (e.g. O²⁻, S²⁻)
- **Group 7** non-metals → **−1** ion (e.g. Cl⁻, Br⁻)

Hint: Group number = electrons lost or gained. Groups 1 and 2 lose. Groups 6 and 7 gain.

Exam Q: "What is the charge on a magnesium ion?" [1 mark]
→ 2+ / Mg²⁺.

Notes link: /notes/c2-ionic-bonding

---

**C2-04** | Topic: Ionic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about what holds ions together in an ionic compound.

A:
- **Strong electrostatic forces** of attraction
- Act between **oppositely charged** ions in **all directions** throughout the lattice

Hint: Positive and negative ions attract each other strongly. The forces act in every direction.

Exam Q: "What holds ions together in an ionic compound?" [2 marks]
→ Strong electrostatic forces (1) + between oppositely charged ions (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-ionic-bonding

---

**C2-05** | Topic: Ionic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **structure of ionic compounds**.

A:
- Ions are arranged in a **giant ionic lattice**
- The lattice is a **regular 3D arrangement** of alternating positive and negative ions
- e.g. sodium chloride — Na⁺ and Cl⁻ arranged in a cube pattern

Hint: Giant lattice = regular, repeating pattern of ions in 3D. Like a huge crystal.

Exam Q: "Describe the structure of an ionic compound such as sodium chloride." [2 marks]
→ Giant lattice / regular arrangement (1) + of oppositely charged ions (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-ionic-bonding

---

**C2-06** | Topic: Ionic Bonding | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **sodium chloride (NaCl)** forms from its elements.

A:
- Each **sodium** atom loses **1 electron** → forms **Na⁺** ion
- Each **chlorine** atom gains **1 electron** → forms **Cl⁻** ion
- One Na reacts with one Cl → **NaCl** held by electrostatic attraction

Hint: Na in Group 1 → loses 1. Cl in Group 7 → gains 1. One-to-one ratio.

Exam Q: "Describe the formation of sodium chloride from its elements." [3 marks]
→ Sodium loses one electron (1) + becomes Na⁺ (1) + chlorine gains one electron / becomes Cl⁻ (1).

## SECTION 2.3 — Properties of Ionic Compounds

---

**C2-07** | Topic: Ionic Compounds | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 properties** of **ionic compounds**.

A:
- **High melting and boiling points** — many strong electrostatic forces to overcome
- **Conduct electricity** when **molten** — ions are free to move
- **Conduct electricity** when **dissolved** in water — ions are free to move
- **Don't conduct** when **solid** — ions are fixed in position

Hint: Solid = no. Molten or dissolved = yes. Hard to melt because of many strong forces.

Exam Q: "Why do ionic compounds conduct electricity when dissolved in water but not when solid?" [2 marks]
→ Ions free to move when dissolved (1) + ions fixed in place in solid (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-ionic-compounds

---

**C2-08** | Topic: Ionic Compounds | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about why ionic compounds have **high melting points**.

A:
- There are **many strong electrostatic forces** throughout the giant lattice
- **Lots of energy** is needed to overcome all these forces

Hint: Millions of bonds in every direction — each one strong. Needs lots of energy to break them all.

Exam Q: "Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?" [2 marks]
→ Many strong electrostatic forces (1) + lots of energy needed to overcome them (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-ionic-compounds

## SECTION 2.4 — Covalent Bonding

---

**C2-09** | Topic: Covalent Bonding | Tier: F

Q: What is **covalent bonding**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- The **sharing** of a pair of electrons
- Between two **non-metal** atoms
- Each atom shares electrons to achieve a **full outer shell**

Hint: Covalent = sharing between non-metals. Both atoms benefit from the shared electrons.

Exam Q: "What is a covalent bond?" [2 marks]
→ Sharing of a pair of electrons (1) + between non-metal atoms (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-covalent-bonding

---

**C2-10** | Topic: Covalent Bonding | Tier: F

Q: Give the **molecular formula** and **number of bonds** for these covalent molecules. *(4 facts)*

A:
- **Hydrogen (H₂)** — 1 single bond (H−H)
- **Chlorine (Cl₂)** — 1 single bond (Cl−Cl)
- **Water (H₂O)** — 2 single bonds (O−H)
- **Methane (CH₄)** — 4 single bonds (C−H)
- **Oxygen (O₂)** — 1 double bond (O=O)

Hint: Count how many bonds each atom needs to fill its outer shell.

Exam Q: "Give the formula of a water molecule." [1 mark]
→ H₂O.

Notes link: /notes/c2-covalent-bonding

## SECTION 2.5 — Simple Molecular Substances

---

**C2-11** | Topic: Simple Molecules | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 properties** of **simple molecular substances**.

A:
- **Low melting and boiling points** — weak **intermolecular forces** between molecules
- Usually **gases or liquids** at room temperature
- **Don't conduct electricity** — no free ions or electrons
- Some are **soluble** in water

Hint: Weak forces between molecules = easy to separate = low melting/boiling points. Can't conduct = no charged particles free to move.

Exam Q: "Why do simple molecular substances have low melting points?" [2 marks]
→ Weak intermolecular forces between molecules (1) + little energy needed to overcome them (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-simple-molecules

---

**C2-12** | Topic: Simple Molecules | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about **intermolecular forces** and how they change with molecular size.

A:
- The forces between molecules are **weak** compared to the bonds within molecules
- As molecules get **larger**, intermolecular forces get **stronger** → higher boiling point

Hint: Bigger molecule = more electrons = stronger forces between molecules = harder to boil.

Exam Q: "Why does the boiling point of halogens increase going down Group 7?" [2 marks]
→ Molecules get larger (1) + stronger intermolecular forces / more energy needed to separate them (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-simple-molecules

## SECTION 2.6 — Giant Covalent Structures

---

**C2-13** | Topic: Giant Covalent | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **giant covalent structures**.

A:
- Contain **many strong covalent bonds** throughout the whole structure
- Have **very high melting points** — lots of energy needed to break all the bonds
- Most **don't conduct electricity** — no free ions or electrons (except graphite)

Hint: Diamond and silicon dioxide = giant covalent. Millions of bonds to break = very high melting point.

Exam Q: "Why do giant covalent substances have very high melting points?" [2 marks]
→ Many strong covalent bonds (1) + lots of energy needed to break them all (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-giant-covalent

---

**C2-14** | Topic: Giant Covalent | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the structure and properties of **diamond**.

A:
- Each carbon atom is bonded to **4 others** in a **tetrahedral** arrangement
- Very **hard** — all bonds must be broken to move atoms
- **Doesn't conduct electricity** — no free electrons or ions

Hint: 4 bonds, very hard, doesn't conduct. Used in cutting tools and drill bits.

Exam Q: "Describe the structure of diamond and give one property that results from it." [3 marks]
→ Each C bonded to 4 others / tetrahedral structure (1) + giant covalent lattice (1) + very hard / high melting point (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-giant-covalent

---

**C2-15** | Topic: Giant Covalent | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about the structure and properties of **graphite**.

A:
- Each carbon atom is bonded to **3 others** in **layers of hexagonal rings**
- Layers can **slide** over each other — makes graphite soft and slippery
- **Conducts electricity** — one **delocalised** electron per carbon atom, free to move
- Used as a **lubricant** and in **electrodes**

Hint: 3 bonds per carbon, layers slide, conducts. Very different from diamond despite both being carbon.

Exam Q: "Why can graphite conduct electricity but diamond cannot?" [2 marks]
→ Graphite has delocalised electrons (1) + that are free to move and carry charge (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-giant-covalent

---

**C2-16** | Topic: Giant Covalent | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about why graphite is **soft and slippery**.

A:
- The carbon atoms are arranged in **layers**
- The layers are held by **weak forces** and can **slide** over each other

Hint: Strong bonds within layers, but weak forces between layers. Layers slide = soft.

Exam Q: "Why is graphite soft enough to be used as a lubricant?" [2 marks]
→ Arranged in layers (1) + weak forces between layers / layers can slide (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-giant-covalent

Q: Give **3 facts** about **graphene**.

A:
- A **single layer** of graphite — just one atom thick
- Has **delocalised electrons** — conducts electricity very well
- Extremely **strong** and very **light**

Hint: Graphene = one layer of graphite. Incredibly thin and strong.

Exam Q: "Give two properties of graphene." [2 marks]

**C2-18** | Topic: Giant Covalent | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about **fullerenes** and **nanotubes**.

A:
- **Fullerenes** are cage-like molecules of carbon (e.g. C₆₀ — Buckminsterfullerene — shaped like a football)
- **Nanotubes** are cylindrical carbon structures — very strong and conduct electricity
- Used in **nanotechnology**, drug delivery and as lubricants

Hint: Buckyballs (C₆₀) and tubes. Both made of hexagonal carbon rings.

**C2-19** | Topic: Giant Covalent | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about why carbon nanotubes **conduct electricity**.

A:
- Each carbon atom forms **3 covalent bonds**
- This leaves **one electron per atom delocalised**
- These delocalised electrons **carry charge** through the structure

Hint: 3 bonds = 1 spare electron per carbon = delocalised = conducts.

Exam Q: "Describe why carbon nanotubes can conduct electricity." [3 marks] *(AQA June 2024, Q08)*
→ Each C forms 3 bonds (1) + one electron per atom is delocalised (1) + delocalised electrons carry charge (1).

## SECTION 2.8 — Metallic Bonding and Alloys

---

**C2-22** | Topic: Metallic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **metallic bonding**.

A:
- Metal atoms lose their **outer electrons**
- These electrons form a **sea of delocalised electrons**
- The delocalised electrons are attracted to the **positive metal ions** — holding the structure together

Hint: Sea of electrons flowing between positive ions. Explains why metals conduct.

Exam Q: "Describe metallic bonding." [3 marks]
→ Sea of delocalised electrons (1) + positive metal ions (1) + electrostatic attraction between them (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-metallic-bonding

---

**C2-23** | Topic: Metallic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 properties** of **metals** and the reason for each.

A:
- **Conduct electricity** — delocalised electrons carry charge through the structure
- **Conduct heat** — delocalised electrons transfer energy through the structure
- **Malleable** — layers of ions can slide over each other without breaking the structure
- **High melting points** — strong metallic bonding throughout

Hint: Delocalised electrons = conduct. Layers slide = malleable. Strong bonding = high melting point.

Exam Q: "Why do metals conduct electricity?" [2 marks]
→ Delocalised electrons (1) + free to move and carry charge through the structure (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-metallic-bonding

---

**C2-24** | Topic: Metallic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: What is an **alloy**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **mixture** of two or more metals (or a metal and a non-metal)
- The atoms are **different sizes** — this distorts the regular layers

Hint: Steel = iron + carbon. Bronze = copper + tin. Different sized atoms.

Exam Q: "What is an alloy?" [1 mark]
→ A mixture of a metal with other elements (metals or non-metals).

Notes link: /notes/c2-alloys

---

**C2-25** | Topic: Metallic Bonding | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about why **alloys are harder** than pure metals.

A:
- In a pure metal, atoms are **the same size** and layers slide easily
- In an alloy, atoms are **different sizes** — the regular pattern is **distorted**
- The layers **can't slide** as easily — so the alloy is **harder**

Hint: Same size = slides freely = soft. Different sizes = layers jam = harder.

Exam Q: "Describe why an alloy is harder than a pure metal." [3 marks] *(AQA June 2024, Q01)*
→ Atoms are different sizes (1) + layers are distorted (1) + layers can't slide easily (1).

Notes link: /notes/c2-alloys

## SECTION 2.10 — Polymers

---

**C2-31** | Topic: Polymers | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the structure and properties of **polymers**.

A:
- Made of very **long chains** of repeating units bonded together
- Have **higher melting points** than simple molecules — longer chains, stronger intermolecular forces
- **Don't conduct electricity** — no free ions or electrons

Hint: Long chains = stronger forces between them = harder to melt than small molecules.

Exam Q: "Why do polymers have higher melting points than simple molecules?" [2 marks]
→ Longer chains (1) + stronger intermolecular forces between chains (1).
