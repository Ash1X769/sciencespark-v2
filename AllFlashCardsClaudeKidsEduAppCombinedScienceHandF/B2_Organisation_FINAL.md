## SECTION 2.3 — Enzymes

---

**B2-07** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What is an **enzyme**? *(4 facts)*

A:
- A **biological catalyst**
- Speeds up **chemical reactions** in living organisms
- Made of **protein**
- Is **not used up** in the reaction

Hint: Biological catalyst — speeds reactions, made of protein, reusable

Exam Q: "What is an enzyme?" [2 marks]
→ Biological catalyst (1) that speeds up chemical reactions (1).

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes

---

**B2-08** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What is the **active site** of an enzyme? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The part of the enzyme where the **substrate binds**
- Has a **specific shape** that only fits one type of substrate

Hint: The lock — only one key (substrate) fits

Exam Q: "Describe the lock and key model of enzyme action." [3 marks]
→ Active site has specific shape (1) + only one substrate fits (1) + enzyme-substrate complex forms (1).

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes

---

**B2-09** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What is the **lock and key model** of enzyme action? *(4 facts)*

A:
- The enzyme has an **active site** with a **specific shape**
- Only a substrate with a **complementary shape** can fit
- An **enzyme-substrate complex** is formed
- Products are released and the **enzyme is unchanged**

Hint: Lock (active site) + Key (substrate) = reaction happens. Lock unchanged after.

Exam Q: "Describe the lock and key model of enzyme action." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes

---

**B2-10** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What is the **optimum temperature** of an enzyme? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The temperature at which the enzyme works at its **fastest rate**
- Most human enzymes have an optimum of around **37°C**

Hint: Body temperature = optimum for most human enzymes

Exam Q: "Describe why enzymes work best at a specific temperature." [2 marks]
→ Optimum temperature = fastest rate (1). Above/below = rate decreases (1).

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes

---

**B2-11** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What happens to an enzyme at **temperatures above** its optimum? *(4 facts)*

A:
- The enzyme **denatures**
- The **active site changes shape**
- The substrate can **no longer fit**
- The reaction **stops**

Hint: Too hot → active site changes shape → substrate won't fit → enzyme doesn't work

Exam Q: "Describe what happens to an enzyme at high temperatures." [3 marks] *(AQA June 2023, Q06.6)*
→ Active site changes shape / enzyme denatures (1) + substrate no longer fits (1) + reaction stops / rate decreases (1).

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes

---

**B2-12** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What happens to enzyme activity at **temperatures below** the optimum? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Particles have less **kinetic energy**
- **Fewer** enzyme-substrate collisions per second
- Rate of reaction **decreases**

Hint: Cold = slow moving particles = fewer collisions = slower reaction

Exam Q: "Describe why a low temperature reduces the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction." [2 marks]
→ Less kinetic energy (1) → fewer collisions / slower reaction rate (1).

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes

---

**B2-13** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What happens to an enzyme if the **pH** moves away from its optimum? *(3 facts)*

A:
- The **active site changes shape**
- The substrate can **no longer fit**
- The enzyme is **denatured**

Hint: Wrong pH = wrong shape = no reaction. Same result as wrong temperature.

Exam Q: "Describe how pH affects the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction." [3 marks] *(AQA June 2022, Q03.7)*
→ Active site changes shape (1) + substrate no longer fits (1) + enzyme denatured at extreme pH (1).

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes

---

**B2-14** | Topic: Enzymes | Tier: F

Q: What is **denaturation** of an enzyme? *(3 facts)*

A:
- The **active site permanently changes shape**
- The substrate can **no longer fit** the active site
- The enzyme can **no longer function**

Hint: Permanent change — not just slowed down. Can't be reversed.

Exam Q: "What is meant by denaturation of an enzyme?" [2 marks]
→ Active site changes shape (1) + permanently / substrate no longer fits (1).

Notes link: /notes/b2-enzymes
