## SECTION 3.1 — Pathogens

---

**B3-01** | Topic: Pathogens | Tier: F

Q: What is a **pathogen**? *(1 fact)*

A:
- A **microorganism** that causes **disease**

Hint: Path = suffering. A pathogen makes you ill.

Exam Q: "What is a pathogen?" [1 mark]
→ Microorganism that causes disease. One mark.

- **Viruses**
- **Fungi**
- **Protists**

- Toxins **damage tissues** and cause symptoms

Hint: Viruses hijack your cells, use them to copy themselves, then destroy them.

Exam Q: "Describe how viruses cause disease." [2 marks]
→ Replicate inside cells (1) + cause cell damage (1).

## SECTION 3.2 — Specific Diseases

---

**B3-05** | Topic: Diseases | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **measles**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Caused by a **virus**
- Spread by **droplets** from sneezes and coughs
- Prevented by **vaccination**

Hint: Virus + droplets + vaccine. Children are vaccinated against measles.

Exam Q: "How is measles spread?" [1 mark]
→ By droplets from sneezes / coughs / breathing.

Notes link: /notes/b3-diseases

---

**B3-06** | Topic: Diseases | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about **HIV**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Caused by a **virus**
- Spread by **sexual contact** or **exchange of body fluids** (e.g. sharing needles)
- **Damages white blood cells** — weakens the immune system
- Treated with **antiretroviral drugs**

Hint: Virus + body fluids + destroys white blood cells + antiretroviral drugs

Exam Q: "Describe how HIV is spread." [2 marks] *(AQA June 2023, Q01.7)*
→ Sexual contact (1) + exchange of body fluids / sharing needles (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-diseases

**B3-07** | Topic: Diseases | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV)**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Caused by a **virus**
- Spread by **direct contact** between plants
- Causes **mottling** (discolouration) of leaves → **reduces photosynthesis**

Hint: Mosaic pattern on leaves + less photosynthesis = less growth

Exam Q: "Give two effects of Tobacco Mosaic Virus on a plant." [2 marks]
→ Discolouration / mottling of leaves (1) + reduced photosynthesis (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-diseases

- Causes: **fever**, stomach cramps, diarrhoea, vomiting
- Prevented by **vaccination of poultry**

**B3-10** | Topic: Diseases | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about **rose black spot**. *(5 facts)*

- Spread by **spores** in **water or wind**

- **Reduces photosynthesis** → reduces growth
- Treated with **fungicides** or removing affected leaves

Hint: Fungus + spores + yellow leaves + fungicide

**B3-11** | Topic: Diseases | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about **malaria**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Caused by a **protist** (Plasmodium)
- Spread by a **vector** — the **mosquito**
- Symptoms: **fever**, can be **fatal**
- Prevented by: stopping mosquitoes **breeding**, using **mosquito nets**

Hint: Protist + mosquito vector + fever + nets/prevention

## SECTION 3.3 — Body Defences

---

**B3-12** | Topic: Body Defences | Tier: F

Q: What are the **4 non-specific defences** the body uses against pathogens? *(4 facts)*

A:
- **Skin** — physical barrier, prevents pathogens entering
- **Nose** — hairs and mucus trap pathogens
- **Trachea/bronchi** — mucus traps pathogens; cilia move mucus away
- **Stomach** — produces **hydrochloric acid** to kill pathogens

Hint: Skin, Nose, Airways, Stomach — the four barriers before pathogens reach your cells

Exam Q: "Give two ways the body defends itself against pathogens before they enter cells." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from the bullets above = 2 marks.

Notes link: /notes/b3-defences

---

**B3-13** | Topic: Body Defences | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about how the **skin** protects against infection. *(2 facts)*

A:
- Acts as a **physical barrier**
- Prevents pathogens from **entering** the body

Hint: Unbroken skin = no way in for pathogens

Exam Q: "Describe how the skin protects against infection." [2 marks]
→ Physical barrier (1) + prevents pathogens entering (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-defences

---

**B3-14** | Topic: Body Defences | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how the **trachea and bronchi** protect against infection. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Lined with **mucus** — traps pathogens
- Contains **cilia** — tiny hairs that beat
- Cilia move the mucus (with trapped pathogens) **up to be swallowed**

Hint: Mucus traps → cilia move → swallowed → destroyed by stomach acid

Exam Q: "Describe how the trachea protects against infection." [3 marks]
→ Mucus traps pathogens (1) + cilia beat (1) + mucus moved up to be swallowed (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-defences

---

**B3-15** | Topic: Body Defences | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about how the **stomach** protects against infection. *(2 facts)*

A:
- Produces **hydrochloric acid**
- The acid **kills** most pathogens in food and drink

Hint: Stomach acid = very low pH = kills most pathogens

Exam Q: "Describe how the stomach acts as a defence against pathogens." [2 marks]
→ Produces hydrochloric acid (1) + kills pathogens (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-defences

## SECTION 3.4 — White Blood Cells

---

**B3-16** | Topic: White Blood Cells | Tier: F

Q: What are the **3 ways** white blood cells defend against pathogens? *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Phagocytosis** — engulf and digest pathogens
- Produce **antibodies** — destroy specific pathogens
- Produce **antitoxins** — neutralise toxins released by bacteria

Hint: Engulf, Antibodies, Antitoxins — EAA

Exam Q: "Describe three ways white blood cells defend the body against pathogens." [3 marks]
→ One mark per bullet. All three needed for full marks.

Notes link: /notes/b3-white-blood-cells

---

**B3-17** | Topic: White Blood Cells | Tier: F

Q: What is **phagocytosis**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- White blood cells (**phagocytes**) **engulf** pathogens
- The pathogen is then **digested** and destroyed

Hint: Phago = eating. Phagocytes eat pathogens.

Exam Q: "Describe how phagocytosis destroys pathogens." [2 marks]
→ Engulf / surround pathogen (1) + digest / destroy it (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-white-blood-cells

---

**B3-18** | Topic: White Blood Cells | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **antibodies**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Produced by white blood cells called **lymphocytes**
- Each antibody has a **specific shape** that matches one type of **antigen**
- They bind to pathogens and **destroy** them or **clump** them together

Hint: Specific antibody + specific antigen = destroyed pathogen

Exam Q: "Describe how antibodies destroy pathogens." [3 marks]
→ Produced by lymphocytes (1) + specific to antigen (1) + bind to and destroy/clump pathogens (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-white-blood-cells

---

**B3-19** | Topic: White Blood Cells | Tier: F

Q: What are **antitoxins** and what do they do? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Produced by white blood cells
- **Neutralise toxins** released by bacteria

Hint: Anti-toxin = against toxins. Cancels out the poison.

Exam Q: "What are antitoxins?" [2 marks]
→ Produced by white blood cells (1) + neutralise bacterial toxins (1).

## SECTION 3.5 — Vaccination

---

**B3-20** | Topic: Vaccination | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about how **vaccination** works. *(4 facts)*

A:
- A **dead or weakened** form of the pathogen is introduced
- Contains **antigens** that trigger white blood cells to produce **antibodies**
- **Memory cells** are formed
- If real pathogen enters later, memory cells produce **large quantities of antibodies rapidly** — before you get ill

Hint: Dead pathogen in → antibodies made → memory cells formed → fast response next time

Exam Q: "Describe how vaccination protects against disease." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet. All four needed for full marks.

Notes link: /notes/b3-vaccination

---

**B3-21** | Topic: Vaccination | Tier: F

Q: What are **memory cells** and why are they important? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Remain in the body **after** the initial immune response
- If the same pathogen enters again, memory cells produce **antibodies rapidly**
- Response is **faster and larger** — you don't get ill

Hint: Memory cells = immunological memory = never forget a pathogen

Exam Q: "Describe why a vaccinated person does not get ill when exposed to a pathogen." [3 marks]
→ Memory cells remain (1) + produce antibodies rapidly (1) + before illness develops (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-vaccination

---

**B3-22** | Topic: Vaccination | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about **herd immunity**. *(2 facts)*

A:
- When enough of the population is vaccinated, the pathogen **cannot spread easily**
- This **protects** unvaccinated individuals in the community

Hint: Enough people immune = pathogen has nowhere to go = everyone safer

Exam Q: "Describe the benefit of vaccinating a large proportion of the population." [2 marks]
→ Pathogen cannot spread / herd immunity (1) + protects unvaccinated people (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-vaccination

## SECTION 3.6 — Antibiotics and Medicines

---

**B3-23** | Topic: Antibiotics | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **antibiotics**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Kill or inhibit the growth of **bacteria**
- **Cannot** kill **viruses**
- Specific antibiotics work against **specific** bacteria

Hint: Antibiotics = bacteria only. Useless against viruses.

Exam Q: "State two facts about antibiotics." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from the bullets above = 2 marks.

Notes link: /notes/b3-medicines

---

**B3-24** | Topic: Antibiotics | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **antibiotic resistance**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Some bacteria **mutate** randomly and become **resistant** to antibiotics
- Resistant bacteria **survive** and **reproduce** — passing on the resistance gene
- Overuse of antibiotics **increases** the number of resistant strains

Hint: Random mutation → resistant bacteria survive → spread → big problem

Exam Q: "Describe how antibiotic resistance develops in bacteria." [3 marks]
→ Random mutation (1) + resistant bacteria survive and reproduce (1) + resistance passes to offspring (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-medicines

---

**B3-25** | Topic: Antibiotics | Tier: F

Q: Why should antibiotics **not** be over-prescribed? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Increases **selection pressure** for resistant strains
- More **antibiotic-resistant** bacteria develop
- Antibiotics become **less effective** over time

Hint: More antibiotic use = more chance for resistant bacteria to take over

Exam Q: "Give one reason why doctors should not prescribe antibiotics unnecessarily." [1 mark] *(AQA June 2022, Q01.6)*
→ To reduce / prevent antibiotic resistance developing.

Notes link: /notes/b3-medicines

**B3-26** | Topic: Antibiotics | Tier: F

Q: What do **painkillers** do? *(2 facts)*

- **Relieve symptoms** of disease

Exam Q: "What is the difference between painkillers and antibiotics?" [2 marks]

## SECTION 3.7 — Drug Development and Clinical Trials

---

**B3-29** | Topic: Clinical Trials | Tier: F

Q: What are the **3 stages** of drug testing before a drug reaches patients? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Stage 1: **Preclinical** — tested on cells and animals for **toxicity** and **efficacy**
- Stage 2: **Healthy volunteers** — very low doses to test for **toxicity**
- Stage 3: **Patients** — larger groups, test for toxicity, **efficacy** and **dose**

Hint: Cells/animals → healthy humans → ill patients. Always in this order.

Exam Q: "Describe the stages of drug testing before a new drug is used on patients." [3 marks] *(AQA June 2023, Q02)*
→ Pre-clinical / cells and animals (1) + healthy volunteers (1) + patients / clinical trials (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-clinical-trials

---

**B3-30** | Topic: Clinical Trials | Tier: F

Q: What is a **placebo** and why is it used? *(3 facts)*

A:
- A substance with **no active ingredient**
- Used to test if effects of a drug are **psychological** or **real**
- The control group receives the placebo — compared to the group receiving the real drug

Hint: Fake drug = placebo. Shows if improvement is real or just belief.

Exam Q: "Describe why a placebo is used in a clinical trial." [2 marks]
→ No active ingredient (1) + to test if effects are psychological / to compare against real drug (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-clinical-trials

---

**B3-31** | Topic: Clinical Trials | Tier: F

Q: What is a **double-blind trial**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Neither the **patient** nor the **researcher** knows who has received the drug or the placebo
- Removes **bias** from the results

Hint: Both sides blind = no bias. Neither patient nor doctor knows who got what.

Exam Q: "What is a double-blind trial and why is it used?" [2 marks]
→ Neither patient nor researcher knows who has drug/placebo (1) + removes bias (1).

Notes link: /notes/b3-clinical-trials

---

**B3-32** | Topic: Clinical Trials | Tier: H

Q: What do **toxicity**, **efficacy** and **dose** mean in drug trials? *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Toxicity** — how **harmful** the drug is / whether it has dangerous side effects
- **Efficacy** — how **effective** the drug is at treating the condition
- **Dose** — the **amount** of drug given to the patient

Hint: Toxic = harmful. Efficacy = effective. Dose = amount.

Exam Q: "State what is meant by the term 'efficacy' in a drug trial." [1 mark]
→ How effective the drug is.

Notes link: /notes/b3-clinical-trials
