## SECTION 4.1 — Atomic Structure

---

**P4-01** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **structure of an atom**.

A:
- A tiny, positively charged **nucleus** at the centre (containing protons and neutrons)
- **Electrons** orbit the nucleus in **shells (energy levels)**
- The atom is mostly **empty space** — the nucleus is tiny compared to the atom

Hint: Nucleus in the middle (positive). Electrons orbiting outside (negative). Mostly empty space.

Exam Q: "Describe the structure of an atom." [3 marks]
→ Nucleus at centre (1) + electrons orbit in shells / energy levels (1) + mostly empty space (1).

**P4-02** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **subatomic particles**.

A:
- **Proton** — charge **+1**, mass **1** — in the **nucleus**
- **Neutron** — charge **0**, mass **1** — in the **nucleus**
- **Electron** — charge **−1**, mass very small — orbiting in **shells**

Hint: Protons and Neutrons in the Nucleus. Electrons outside.

Exam Q: "State the charge and relative mass of a proton." [2 marks]
→ Charge +1 (1) + relative mass 1 (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-atomic-structure

**P4-03** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 steps** in the **development of the atomic model**.

A:
- **Plum pudding** — a ball of positive charge with electrons embedded in it (Thomson)
- **Nuclear model** — Rutherford's alpha scattering experiment showed a tiny positive nucleus
- **Bohr model** — electrons orbit the nucleus at specific distances (**energy levels/shells**)

Hint: Plum pudding → Nuclear (Rutherford) → Shells (Bohr). Each needed new evidence.

Exam Q: "Describe how the atomic model changed from the plum pudding model to the nuclear model." [3 marks]
→ Alpha particles fired at gold foil (1) + most passed through / some deflected back (1) + showed concentrated positive nucleus (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-atomic-model

---

**P4-04** | Topic: Atomic Structure | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about **electron energy levels**.

A:
- Electrons can **absorb radiation** and move to a **higher energy level** (further from nucleus)
- Electrons can **emit radiation** and move to a **lower energy level** (closer to nucleus)

Hint: Absorb = move out. Emit = move in. Energy levels = shells.

Exam Q: "Describe what happens to an electron when it absorbs radiation." [2 marks]
→ Moves to a higher energy level / further from nucleus (1) + absorbs energy from radiation (1).

## SECTION 4.2 — Radioactive Decay

---

**P4-05** | Topic: Radioactive Decay | Tier: F

Q: What is **radioactive decay**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- When an **unstable nucleus** changes to become more **stable**
- By **emitting radiation**
- The process is **random** — we can't predict when any individual nucleus will decay

Hint: Unstable nucleus → emits radiation → becomes more stable. Can't predict when it happens.

Exam Q: "Describe radioactive decay." [3 marks]
→ Unstable nucleus (1) + emits radiation (1) + to become more stable / process is random (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-radioactive-decay

---

**P4-06** | Topic: Radioactive Decay | Tier: F

Q: What is **activity** and what is it measured in? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **rate** at which nuclear decay occurs
- Measured in **becquerels (Bq)** — 1 Bq = 1 decay per second

Hint: Activity = how many decays per second. Higher activity = more unstable sample.

Exam Q: "What is meant by the activity of a radioactive source?" [2 marks]
→ Rate of radioactive decay (1) + measured in becquerels / decays per second (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-radioactive-decay

---

**P4-07** | Topic: Radioactive Decay | Tier: F

Q: What is **half-life**? Give **2 definitions**. *(2 facts)*

A:
- The time it takes for the **number of radioactive nuclei** in a sample to **halve**
- Also: the time for the **count rate (or activity)** to fall to **half** its initial value

Hint: After one half-life, half the nuclei have decayed. After two, a quarter remain.

Exam Q: "What is meant by half-life?" [2 marks]
→ Time for number of nuclei / activity to halve (1) + of a radioactive isotope (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-half-life

---

**P4-08** | Topic: Radioactive Decay | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 steps** for **finding the half-life** from a graph.

A:
- Find the **initial count rate**
- **Halve** that value
- Draw a horizontal line across and then **down** to the time axis — this is the half-life

Hint: Start at the top count rate → halve it → read off the time. Can repeat to check.

Exam Q: "Describe how to find the half-life of an isotope from a count rate graph." [3 marks]
→ Find initial count rate (1) + halve the value (1) + read off the corresponding time from the graph (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-half-life

## SECTION 4.3 — Types of Radiation

---

**P4-09** | Topic: Radiation Types | Tier: F

Q: List the **4 types** of nuclear radiation. *(4 facts)*

A:
- **Alpha (α)** — 2 protons + 2 neutrons (helium nucleus)
- **Beta (β)** — a fast-moving **electron** from the nucleus
- **Gamma (γ)** — an **electromagnetic wave** from the nucleus
- **Neutron radiation** — emitted in nuclear fission reactions

Hint: Alpha (heavy, charged), Beta (electron), Gamma (electromagnetic wave), Neutron.

Exam Q: "Name the three types of nuclear radiation." [3 marks]
→ Alpha (1) + beta (1) + gamma (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-radiation

---

**P4-10** | Topic: Radiation Types | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **alpha radiation**.

A:
- A **helium nucleus** — 2 protons and 2 neutrons
- **Very high** ionising power
- **Short range** in air — stopped by a **few cm of air** or a sheet of **paper**

Hint: Heavy, charged, can't travel far. Most dangerous inside the body. Stopped by paper.

Exam Q: "Give two properties of alpha radiation." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: 2 protons + 2 neutrons / very ionising / short range / stopped by paper.

Notes link: /notes/p4-radiation

---

**P4-11** | Topic: Radiation Types | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **beta radiation**.

A:
- A **fast-moving electron** emitted from the nucleus
- **Medium** ionising power
- Range of about **1 metre** in air — stopped by a few mm of **aluminium**

Hint: Electron from nucleus. Medium range. Stopped by aluminium sheet.

Exam Q: "Give two properties of beta radiation." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: fast electron / medium ionising power / range ~1 m / stopped by aluminium.

Notes link: /notes/p4-radiation

---

**P4-12** | Topic: Radiation Types | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **gamma radiation**.

A:
- An **electromagnetic wave** — no mass or charge
- **Low** ionising power
- **Unlimited range** in air — stopped by **several cm of lead** or thick concrete

Hint: No mass, no charge. Travels forever unless absorbed by lead. Least ionising.

Exam Q: "Give two properties of gamma radiation." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: electromagnetic wave / no mass or charge / low ionising power / unlimited range / stopped by lead.

Notes link: /notes/p4-radiation

---

**P4-13** | Topic: Radiation Types | Tier: F

Q: What is **ionising radiation** and why is it dangerous? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Radiation that can **remove electrons** from atoms to form **ions**
- If ionisation occurs in **DNA**, it can cause **mutations** leading to **cancer**

Hint: Knocks electrons off atoms = ions = can damage DNA = cancer risk.

Exam Q: "Explain why ionising radiation is dangerous to humans." [2 marks]
→ Can ionise atoms / remove electrons (1) + if this happens in DNA it can cause mutations / cancer (1).

## SECTION 4.4 — Contamination and Irradiation

---

**P4-14** | Topic: Contamination & Irradiation | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **contamination** and **irradiation**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Contamination** — radioactive material **gets onto or into** the body (e.g. inhaled or swallowed)
- **Irradiation** — the body is **exposed to radiation** from an external source — the body does **not** become radioactive

Hint: Contamination = radioactive stuff ON/IN you. Irradiation = radiation hits you from outside. Contamination is more dangerous.

Exam Q: "Explain the difference between contamination and irradiation." [2 marks]
→ Contamination = radioactive material on/in body (1) + irradiation = exposed to external source / body doesn't become radioactive (1).

Notes link: /notes/p4-contamination

---

**P4-15** | Topic: Contamination & Irradiation | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about **peer review** in science.

A:
- The checking of **scientific results** by other **independent scientists**
- Helps detect **false claims** and establish which results should be considered **valid**

Hint: Other scientists check your work before it's published. Helps weed out mistakes and bias.

Exam Q: "What is peer review and why is it important?" [2 marks]
→ Checking of results by other scientists (1) + helps detect false claims / establish valid results (1).
