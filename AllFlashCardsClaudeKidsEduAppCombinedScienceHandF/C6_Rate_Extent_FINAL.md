## SECTION 6.1 — Rate of Reaction

---

**C6-01** | Topic: Rate of Reaction | Tier: F

Q: What is the **rate of reaction**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- How **quickly** a reaction takes place
- Measured as: **mean rate = amount of reactant used ÷ time taken** OR **amount of product formed ÷ time taken**

Hint: Fast = high rate. Slow = low rate. Rate = how much changes per second.

Exam Q: "State the equation for mean rate of reaction." [1 mark]
→ Mean rate = amount of reactant used / product formed ÷ time taken.

Q: List the **5 factors** that affect the rate of reaction. *(5 facts)*

A:

- **Catalyst**
- **Light** (for some reactions e.g. photosynthesis, photography)

Hint: Temp, Concentration, Surface area, Catalyst, Light — TCSCL.

**C6-03** | Topic: Rate of Reaction | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **collision theory**.

A:
- Reactions only happen when particles **collide** with each other
- The collision must have **enough energy** — at least the **activation energy**
- More **frequent** and more **energetic** collisions = faster rate of reaction

Hint: Must collide AND have enough energy. Both conditions needed.

Exam Q: "Use collision theory to describe why increasing temperature increases the rate of reaction." [3 marks]
→ Particles have more kinetic energy (1) + move faster / collide more frequently (1) + more collisions have enough energy / exceed activation energy (1).

**C6-04** | Topic: Rate of Reaction | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **increasing temperature** affects the rate of reaction.

A:
- Particles move **faster** — have more **kinetic energy**
- **More frequent** collisions per second
- More collisions have energy **exceeding** the activation energy

Hint: Hotter = faster particles = more frequent + more energetic collisions = faster rate.

Exam Q: "Describe how increasing temperature increases the rate of reaction." [3 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

**C6-05** | Topic: Rate of Reaction | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **increasing concentration** affects the rate of reaction.

A:
- More particles in the **same volume**
- Particles are **closer together**
- **More frequent** collisions per second → faster rate

Hint: More concentrated = more crowded = particles bump into each other more often.

Exam Q: "Describe how increasing concentration increases the rate of reaction." [2 marks]
→ More particles in same volume (1) + more frequent collisions / faster rate (1).

**C6-06** | Topic: Rate of Reaction | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **increasing surface area** affects the rate of reaction.

A:
- More of the solid is **exposed** to the other reactant
- More particles are available for **collisions**
- **Faster rate** of reaction

Hint: Smaller pieces = bigger surface area = more exposed = more collisions.

Exam Q: "Why does powdered marble react faster with acid than large marble chips?" [2 marks]
→ Larger surface area (1) + more frequent collisions / more particles exposed to acid (1).

- Increase the rate of reaction
- Are **not used up** — not included in the equation
- Work by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a **lower activation energy**

Hint: Lower activation energy = more collisions succeed = faster rate. Not used up.

## SECTION 6.2 — Measuring Rate of Reaction (Required Practical)

---

**C6-09** | Topic: Required Practical | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 methods** for **measuring the rate of reaction** in the lab.

A:
- Measure the **volume of gas** produced over time (using a gas syringe)
- Measure the **change in mass** over time (if a gas escapes)
- Measure the time for a **precipitate to form** (disappearing cross experiment)

Hint: Gas collected, mass lost, cross disappears. Three methods — all measure how fast products form.

Exam Q: "Describe one method to measure the rate of the reaction between calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid." [2 marks]
→ Any valid method (1) + clear measurement described (1).

Notes link: /notes/c6-required-practical

---

**C6-10** | Topic: Required Practical | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** of the **disappearing cross** experiment.

A:
- Place a conical flask on top of a piece of paper with a **cross** drawn on it
- Mix **sodium thiosulfate** and **hydrochloric acid** in the flask
- A **yellow precipitate** of sulfur forms — solution becomes cloudy
- Record the time until the **cross can no longer be seen** through the flask

Hint: Flask on cross → mix reactants → sulfur forms → time until cross disappears.

Exam Q: "Describe the disappearing cross experiment to measure rate of reaction." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

Notes link: /notes/c6-required-practical

**C6-11** | Topic: Required Practical | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about interpreting a **rate of reaction graph** (mass or volume vs time).

A:
- The **steeper** the gradient at any point, the **faster** the rate at that point
- The graph becomes **flat** (horizontal) when the reaction has **stopped**

Hint: Steep slope = fast. Flat line = stopped. Steepest at the start (most reactant present).

Exam Q: "Describe what the shape of the graph tells you about the rate of reaction over time." [2 marks]
→ Steeper gradient = faster rate (1) + becomes flat when reaction stops / reactant used up (1).

## SECTION 6.3 — Reversible Reactions

---

**C6-12** | Topic: Reversible Reactions | Tier: F

Q: What is a **reversible reaction**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A reaction where the **products can react** to reform the **reactants**
- Shown with a **⇌** (double arrow) in the equation

Hint: Goes both ways. Products can turn back into reactants.

Exam Q: "What does the symbol ⇌ indicate in a chemical equation?" [1 mark]
→ The reaction is reversible.

Notes link: /notes/c6-reversible-reactions

---

**C6-13** | Topic: Reversible Reactions | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about energy in **reversible reactions**.

A:
- If the forward reaction is **exothermic**, the reverse reaction is **endothermic**
- The same amount of energy is transferred in **both directions**

Hint: Reverse = opposite energy direction. What one gives out, the other takes in.

Exam Q: "A reaction is exothermic in the forward direction. What can you say about the reverse reaction?" [1 mark]
→ The reverse reaction is endothermic / takes in the same amount of energy.

Notes link: /notes/c6-reversible-reactions

**C6-14** | Topic: Reversible Reactions | Tier: H

Q: What is **dynamic equilibrium**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Reached in a **closed system** when the reaction is reversible
- The **forward** and **reverse** reactions occur at **exactly the same rate**
- The concentrations of reactants and products **remain constant** (but not necessarily equal)

Hint: Both reactions still happening — just at the same rate. Like a see-saw perfectly balanced.

Exam Q: "What is meant by dynamic equilibrium?" [3 marks]
→ Forward and reverse reactions occur at same rate (1) + in a closed system (1) + concentrations remain constant (1).

Notes link: /notes/c6-equilibrium

**C6-15** | Topic: Reversible Reactions | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about **Le Chatelier's Principle**.

A:
- If a change is made to a system at equilibrium, the system **responds to oppose** the change
- The equilibrium position **shifts** to reduce the effect of the change

Hint: System fights back against changes. Think of it as the system trying to restore balance.

Exam Q: "State Le Chatelier's Principle." [2 marks]
→ If a change is made to a system at equilibrium (1) + the system responds to oppose / counteract the change (1).

Notes link: /notes/c6-equilibrium

---

**C6-16** | Topic: Reversible Reactions | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **temperature** affects the equilibrium position.

A:
- Increasing temperature shifts equilibrium towards the **endothermic** direction
- Decreasing temperature shifts equilibrium towards the **exothermic** direction
- This is because the system responds to **oppose** the temperature change

Hint: Higher temp → system tries to cool down → favours endothermic side.

Exam Q: "A forward reaction is exothermic. What happens to the equilibrium position when temperature is increased?" [1 mark]
→ Shifts to the left / towards reactants / in the endothermic direction.

Notes link: /notes/c6-equilibrium

---

**C6-17** | Topic: Reversible Reactions | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **concentration** affects the equilibrium position.

A:
- Increasing concentration of **reactants** shifts equilibrium to the **right** (more products form)
- Increasing concentration of **products** shifts equilibrium to the **left** (more reactants form)

Hint: Add more of something → system uses it up → shifts away from what you added.

Exam Q: "What happens to the equilibrium position if more reactant is added?" [1 mark]
→ Shifts to the right / towards products.

Notes link: /notes/c6-equilibrium

---

**C6-18** | Topic: Reversible Reactions | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **pressure** affects equilibrium position (gases only).

A:
- Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium towards the side with **fewer moles of gas**
- Decreasing pressure shifts equilibrium towards the side with **more moles of gas**

Hint: Higher pressure → system tries to reduce pressure → fewer gas molecules side wins.

Exam Q: "A reaction has 2 moles of gas on the left and 1 mole of gas on the right. What happens when pressure is increased?" [1 mark]
→ Shifts to the right / towards products / side with fewer moles of gas.
