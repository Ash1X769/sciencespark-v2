## SECTION 4.1 — Key Terms

---

**C4-01** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is an **acid**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A chemical that dissolves in water to produce **H⁺ ions**
- Examples: **hydrochloric acid (HCl)**, **sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄)**, **nitric acid (HNO₃)**

Hint: Acid = H⁺ ions in solution. Sour taste, pH below 7.

Exam Q: "What ions does an acid produce in water?" [1 mark]
→ H⁺ / hydrogen ions.

Notes link: /notes/c4-key-terms

---

**C4-02** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between a **base** and an **alkali**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **base** is a substance that reacts with acids and neutralises them (e.g. metal oxides, metal hydroxides, metal carbonates)
- An **alkali** is a base that **dissolves in water** — produces **OH⁻ ions** in solution

Hint: All alkalis are bases. Not all bases are alkalis. Alkali = dissolves in water.

Exam Q: "What ions does an alkali produce in water?" [1 mark]
→ OH⁻ / hydroxide ions.

Notes link: /notes/c4-key-terms

---

**C4-03** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the **pH scale**.

A:
- **pH 0–6** = **acidic** (lower pH = stronger acid / more H⁺ ions)
- **pH 7** = **neutral**
- **pH 8–14** = **alkaline** (higher pH = stronger alkali / more OH⁻ ions)

Hint: Low = acidic. 7 = neutral. High = alkaline.

Exam Q: "What is the pH of a neutral solution?" [1 mark]
→ pH 7.

Notes link: /notes/c4-key-terms

---

**C4-04** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **pH changes** with concentration of H⁺ ions.

A:
- A decrease of **one pH unit** = a **10-fold increase** in H⁺ ion concentration
- The pH scale is **logarithmic** — not linear

Hint: pH 2 has 10× more H⁺ ions than pH 3. pH 1 has 100× more than pH 3.

Exam Q: "A solution has pH 2. The acid is diluted so its concentration decreases by a factor of 100. What is the new pH?" [1 mark] *(AQA June 2024, Q04)*
→ pH increases by 2 → new pH = **4**.

Notes link: /notes/c4-key-terms

---

**C4-05** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: H

Q: What is the difference between a **strong acid** and a **weak acid**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **strong acid** is **completely ionised** in solution (e.g. HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃)
- A **weak acid** is only **partially ionised** in solution (e.g. ethanoic acid, citric acid)

Hint: Strong = 100% split into ions. Weak = partly split. Same concentration but different pH.

Exam Q: "What is meant by a strong acid?" [1 mark] *(AQA June 2024, Q04)*
→ Completely ionised in aqueous solution.

Notes link: /notes/c4-key-terms

## SECTION 4.2 — Reactivity Series

---

**C4-06** | Topic: Reactivity Series | Tier: F

Q: List the **reactivity series** from most to least reactive. *(1 fact)*

A:
- **K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, (C), Zn, Fe, (H), Cu, Ag, Au**
- Carbon and hydrogen are included for comparison — they're not metals

Hint: Katie Never Caught My Anxious Cat's Zebra Fighting Her Cute Silver Goldfish
K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, C, Zn, Fe, H, Cu, Ag, Au

Exam Q: "Place these metals in order of reactivity, most reactive first: iron, copper, zinc." [1 mark]
→ Zinc, iron, copper.

Notes link: /notes/c4-reactivity-series

---

**C4-07** | Topic: Reactivity Series | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **potassium, sodium and lithium** react with **water**.

A:
- All three react **vigorously** with cold water
- Products: **metal hydroxide** + **hydrogen** gas
- Reactivity increases down Group 1: **lithium < sodium < potassium**

Hint: K fizzes and burns with a lilac flame. Na melts and fizzes rapidly. Li fizzes steadily.

Exam Q: "What are the products when sodium reacts with water?" [2 marks]
→ Sodium hydroxide (1) + hydrogen (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-reactivity-series

---

**C4-08** | Topic: Reactivity Series | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **alkali metals** react with **oxygen**.

A:
- React with oxygen to form **metal oxides**
- Word equation: **metal + oxygen → metal oxide**
- e.g. **4Li + O₂ → 2Li₂O** (lithium oxide)

Hint: Metal + oxygen = metal oxide. All Group 1 metals tarnish quickly in air.

Exam Q: "Write a word equation for the reaction of sodium with oxygen." [1 mark]
→ Sodium + oxygen → sodium oxide.

Notes link: /notes/c4-reactivity-series

---

**C4-09** | Topic: Reactivity Series | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **alkali metals** react with **chlorine**.

A:
- React with chlorine to form **metal chlorides**
- Word equation: **metal + chlorine → metal chloride**
- e.g. **2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl** (sodium chloride)

Hint: Metal + chlorine = metal chloride. Forms an ionic salt.

Exam Q: "Write a word equation for the reaction of lithium with chlorine." [1 mark]
→ Lithium + chlorine → lithium chloride.

Notes link: /notes/c4-reactivity-series

---

**C4-10** | Topic: Reactivity Series | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how metals react with **dilute acids**.

A:
- Metals **above hydrogen** in the reactivity series react with dilute acids
- Products: a **salt** + **hydrogen** gas
- More reactive metals react more **vigorously**

Hint: Metal + acid → salt + hydrogen. Copper and below won't react.

Exam Q: "Write a word equation for the reaction of zinc with hydrochloric acid." [1 mark]
→ Zinc + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + hydrogen.

Notes link: /notes/c4-reactivity-series

---

**C4-11** | Topic: Reactivity Series | Tier: F

Q: What is a **displacement reaction**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **more reactive** metal displaces a **less reactive** metal from its compound
- e.g. iron + copper sulfate → iron sulfate + copper

Hint: More reactive kicks out the less reactive one from its compound.

Exam Q: "Iron filings are added to copper sulfate solution. Describe what you would observe." [2 marks]
→ Copper is deposited on the iron (1) + solution turns from blue to pale green (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-reactivity-series

## SECTION 4.3 — Oxidation and Reduction

---

**C4-12** | Topic: Redox | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 definitions** of **oxidation**.

A:
- **Gain** of oxygen
- (HT) **Loss** of electrons

Hint: OIL — Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons). Or simply: gaining oxygen.

Exam Q: "In the reaction 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, which substance is oxidised and why?" [2 marks]
→ Magnesium (1) + because it gains oxygen / loses electrons (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-redox

---

**C4-13** | Topic: Redox | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 definitions** of **reduction**.

A:
- **Loss** of oxygen
- (HT) **Gain** of electrons

Hint: RIG — Reduction Is Gain (of electrons). Or simply: losing oxygen. OILRIG.

Exam Q: "In the reaction CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O, which substance is reduced and why?" [2 marks]
→ Copper oxide (1) + because it loses oxygen / gains electrons (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-redox

---

**C4-14** | Topic: Redox | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about **redox reactions**.

A:
- Both **oxidation and reduction** occur **simultaneously**
- One substance is **oxidised** while another is **reduced**

Hint: Redox = REDuction + OXidation at the same time. Electrons are transferred from one to the other.

Exam Q: "In the reaction: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Identify what is oxidised and what is reduced." [2 marks]
→ CO is oxidised / gains oxygen (1) + Fe₂O₃ is reduced / loses oxygen (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-redox

## SECTION 4.4 — Extraction of Metals

---

**C4-15** | Topic: Extraction | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **extraction method** depends on reactivity.

A:
- Very **unreactive** metals (e.g. gold) are found **native** — uncombined in the Earth
- Metals **less reactive than carbon** are extracted by **reduction with carbon**
- Metals **more reactive than carbon** are extracted by **electrolysis**

Hint: Gold found freely. Iron reduced by carbon. Aluminium needs electrolysis.

Exam Q: "Why is aluminium extracted by electrolysis rather than reduction with carbon?" [1 mark]
→ Aluminium is more reactive than carbon / can't be reduced by carbon.

## SECTION 4.5 — Reactions of Acids

---

**C4-16** | Topic: Reactions of Acids | Tier: F

Q: What is **neutralisation**? Give the **ionic equation**. *(2 facts)*

A:
- A reaction between an **acid** and a **base** that produces a **neutral solution**
- Ionic equation: **H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O**

Hint: H⁺ from acid + OH⁻ from alkali → water. Always the same ionic equation.

Exam Q: "Write the ionic equation for neutralisation." [1 mark] *(AQA June 2024, Q04)*
→ H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O.

## SECTION 4.6 — Making a Soluble Salt (Required Practical)

---

**C4-22** | Topic: Making Salts | Tier: F

Q: Give **6 steps** for making a **soluble salt** from an acid and an insoluble solid.

A:
- Add the **solid** (metal, metal oxide or metal carbonate) to the **acid** in a beaker
- Stir and warm gently — add solid until **no more dissolves** (excess solid)
- **Filter** to remove the excess solid
- **Evaporate** some of the water from the filtrate to concentrate it
- Leave to **crystallise**
- Remove remaining water in a desiccator or oven to **dry** the crystals

Hint: Add excess → filter → evaporate → crystallise → dry.

Exam Q: "Describe how to make pure crystals of copper sulfate from copper oxide and sulfuric acid." [4 marks]
→ Any 4 from the steps above, in correct order = 4 marks.

## SECTION 4.7 — Electrolysis

---

**C4-23** | Topic: Electrolysis | Tier: F

Q: What is **electrolysis**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The process of **splitting an ionic compound** using **electricity**
- Requires an **electrolyte** — an ionic compound that is **molten or dissolved** in water

Hint: Electricity splits compounds. Ions must be free to move.

Exam Q: "What is electrolysis?" [2 marks]
→ Splitting an ionic compound using electricity (1) + electrolyte must be molten or dissolved (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-electrolysis

---

**C4-24** | Topic: Electrolysis | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** each about the **cathode** and **anode**.

A:
- **Cathode** = **negative** electrode — **positive ions** (cations) move towards it — **reduced** (gain electrons)
- **Anode** = **positive** electrode — **negative ions** (anions) move towards it — **oxidised** (lose electrons)

Hint: CAThode — CATions (positive) go there. Cathode = negative. ANode = ANions (negative) go there.

Exam Q: "What is formed at the cathode when an ionic compound is electrolysed?" [1 mark]
→ The metal / positive ion is discharged / reduced at the cathode.

Notes link: /notes/c4-electrolysis

---

**C4-25** | Topic: Electrolysis | Tier: F

Q: What is discharged at each electrode when a **molten ionic compound** is electrolysed? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Cathode**: the **metal** is produced
- **Anode**: the **non-metal** is produced

Hint: Molten = simple. Metal at cathode, non-metal at anode. e.g. molten NaCl → Na at cathode, Cl₂ at anode.

Exam Q: "What is formed at each electrode when molten lead bromide is electrolysed?" [2 marks]
→ Lead at cathode (1) + bromine at anode (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-electrolysis

---

**C4-26** | Topic: Electrolysis | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about what is discharged at each electrode when an **aqueous solution** is electrolysed.

A:
- **Cathode**: metal is produced if the metal is **less reactive than hydrogen** — otherwise **hydrogen** is produced
- **Anode**: **oxygen** is produced — UNLESS the solution contains **halide ions** (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻) — then the **halogen** is produced instead
- This is because water also provides H⁺ and OH⁻ ions that compete at the electrodes

Hint: Aqueous = more complex. Hydrogen at cathode if metal more reactive than H. Oxygen at anode unless halide ions present.

Exam Q: "State what is produced at each electrode during electrolysis of dilute sulfuric acid." [2 marks]
→ Hydrogen at cathode (1) + oxygen at anode (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-electrolysis

---

**C4-27** | Topic: Electrolysis | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about the electrolysis of **aluminium oxide**.

A:
- Aluminium oxide is dissolved in **cryolite** to **lower its melting point** — saves energy costs
- **Aluminium** forms at the cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
- **Oxygen** forms at the anode — reacts with the **carbon anode** forming CO₂ — anode must be **replaced regularly**

Hint: Cryolite lowers melting point → cheaper. Al at cathode. O at anode burns away the carbon electrode.

Exam Q: "Why do the carbon anodes need to be replaced regularly during electrolysis of aluminium oxide?" [3 marks] *(AQA June 2024, Q06)*
→ Oxygen is produced at the anode (1) + reacts with the carbon electrode (1) + forms CO₂ / electrode burns away (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-electrolysis

---

**C4-28** | Topic: Electrolysis | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about the electrolysis of **brine** (sodium chloride solution).

A:
- **Hydrogen** gas is produced at the cathode
- **Chlorine** gas is produced at the anode (because Cl⁻ ions are present)
- **Sodium hydroxide** solution remains

Hint: Brine → hydrogen (cathode) + chlorine (anode) + sodium hydroxide. All three are useful products.

Exam Q: "State the three products of the electrolysis of brine and give one use of each." [6 marks]
→ Hydrogen (1) + use e.g. fuel / margarine (1). Chlorine (1) + use e.g. bleach / PVC (1). Sodium hydroxide (1) + use e.g. soap / paper (1).

Notes link: /notes/c4-electrolysis
