## SECTION 7.1 — Key Terms

---

**B7-01** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is an **ecosystem**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The interaction of a **community** of living organisms
- With the **non-living** (abiotic) parts of their environment

Hint: Living things + their non-living surroundings = ecosystem

Exam Q: "What is an ecosystem?" [2 marks]
→ Community of organisms (1) + interacting with non-living environment (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-key-terms

---

**B7-02** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is a **habitat**? *(1 fact)*

A:
- The **area** in which an organism **lives**

Hint: Habitat = home. Where an organism is found.

Exam Q: "What is a habitat?" [1 mark]
→ The area where an organism lives.

Notes link: /notes/b7-key-terms

---

**B7-03** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is a **community**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Two or more different species** living in the same ecosystem
- A **stable community** is one where population sizes remain **fairly constant**

Hint: All the different species in one place = community

Exam Q: "What is meant by a community?" [2 marks]
→ Two or more species in an ecosystem (1) + stable = populations remain constant (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-key-terms

---

**B7-04** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is a **population**? *(1 fact)*

A:
- The **total number** of organisms of **one species** in an ecosystem

Hint: All the rabbits in a field = one population

Exam Q: "What is a population?" [1 mark]
→ Total number of one species in an ecosystem.

Notes link: /notes/b7-key-terms

---

**B7-05** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **biodiversity**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **variety** of all different **species** of organisms on Earth
- Or within a particular **ecosystem**

Hint: More different species = higher biodiversity

Exam Q: "What is biodiversity?" [1 mark]
→ Variety of different species on Earth / in an ecosystem.

Notes link: /notes/b7-key-terms

---

**B7-06** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **interdependence**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Each species in a community **depends on other species**
- For food, shelter, **pollination** and other needs

Hint: Everything connected. Remove one species and others are affected.

Exam Q: "What is meant by interdependence?" [2 marks]
→ Species depend on each other (1) + for food / shelter / pollination etc. (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-key-terms

## SECTION 7.3 — Adaptations

---

**B7-12** | Topic: Adaptations | Tier: F

Q: What are the **3 types** of adaptation? *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Structural** — a body part that helps survival (e.g. thick fat layer in polar bears)
- **Functional** — how the body works to help survival (e.g. camels don't sweat)
- **Behavioural** — a behaviour that helps survival (e.g. desert rats stay underground in heat)

Hint: Structure (body parts), Function (body processes), Behaviour (what it does)

Exam Q: "Give two types of adaptation and an example of each." [4 marks]
→ Any 2 types (1 each) + correct example for each (1 each).

Notes link: /notes/b7-adaptations

---

**B7-13** | Topic: Adaptations | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 structural adaptations** of a **polar bear**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Thick layer of fat** for insulation
- **White fur** for camouflage in snow
- **Large paws** to spread weight on ice

Hint: Fat = warm, white = hidden, large paws = doesn't sink

Exam Q: "Describe how a polar bear is adapted to survive in cold conditions." [3 marks]
→ Any 3 structural adaptations with a reason = 3 marks.

Notes link: /notes/b7-adaptations

---

**B7-14** | Topic: Adaptations | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 adaptations** of a **camel** to survive in a **desert**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Large fat store** in hump — not evenly distributed so less insulation
- **Doesn't sweat** much — conserves water (functional adaptation)
- **Large, flat feet** — spread weight on sand

Hint: Fat hump, little sweat, flat feet

Exam Q: "Describe how a camel is adapted to survive in a desert." [3 marks]
→ Any 3 adaptations with reason = 3 marks.

Notes link: /notes/b7-adaptations

## SECTION 7.4 — Food Chains and Predator-Prey

---

**B7-15** | Topic: Food Chains | Tier: F

Q: What is a **producer**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- An organism at the **start** of a food chain
- Produces **glucose** through **photosynthesis**

Hint: Producers make their own food. Always a plant or algae.

Exam Q: "What is a producer?" [2 marks]
→ Start of a food chain (1) + produces glucose through photosynthesis (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-food-chains

---

**B7-16** | Topic: Food Chains | Tier: F

Q: What do the **arrows** in a food chain represent? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **transfer of energy** from one organism to the next
- Arrow points in the direction **energy flows** — from eaten to eater

Hint: Arrow = energy moving. Not "eats" — "energy flows to"

Exam Q: "What do the arrows in a food chain represent?" [1 mark]
→ Transfer of energy / direction of energy flow.

Notes link: /notes/b7-food-chains

---

**B7-17** | Topic: Food Chains | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** of a **predator-prey cycle**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Prey population **increases**
- More food available — predator population **increases**
- More predators — prey population **decreases**
- Less prey — predator population **decreases** — cycle restarts

Hint: Prey up → predators up → prey down → predators down → repeat

Exam Q: "Describe the predator-prey cycle." [4 marks] *(AQA June 2022, Q05)*
→ One mark per bullet above.

## SECTION 7.5 — The Carbon Cycle

---

**B7-18** | Topic: Carbon Cycle | Tier: F

Q: What are the **4 processes** in the carbon cycle? *(4 facts)*

A:
- **Photosynthesis** — plants absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere
- **Respiration** — all organisms release CO₂
- **Decay** — microorganisms decompose dead organisms, releasing CO₂
- **Combustion** — burning fossil fuels releases CO₂

Hint: Photo in, Respire out, Decay out, Combust out

Exam Q: "Give two processes that return CO₂ to the atmosphere." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: respiration / decay / combustion.

Notes link: /notes/b7-carbon-cycle

---

**B7-19** | Topic: Carbon Cycle | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **photosynthesis** affects the carbon cycle. *(2 facts)*

A:
- Plants **absorb CO₂** from the atmosphere
- CO₂ is used to make **glucose** — carbon is stored in the plant

Hint: Photosynthesis removes CO₂ from the atmosphere. Carbon locked in plants.

Exam Q: "Describe the role of photosynthesis in the carbon cycle." [2 marks]
→ Absorbs CO₂ from atmosphere (1) + carbon stored in plant (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-carbon-cycle

---

**B7-20** | Topic: Carbon Cycle | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **decomposers** affect the carbon cycle. *(2 facts)*

A:
- Bacteria and fungi **break down** dead organisms
- They **respire** — releasing CO₂ back into the atmosphere

Hint: Decomposers = recyclers. They break things down and release CO₂.

Exam Q: "Describe the role of decomposers in the carbon cycle." [2 marks]
→ Break down dead organisms (1) + release CO₂ through respiration (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-carbon-cycle

---

**B7-21** | Topic: Carbon Cycle | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 facts** about how **combustion** affects the carbon cycle. *(2 facts)*

A:
- Burning **fossil fuels** releases CO₂ stored over **millions of years**
- This **increases** the concentration of CO₂ in the atmosphere

Hint: Burning fuels releases ancient stored carbon. Adds extra CO₂.

Exam Q: "Describe how combustion of fossil fuels affects the carbon cycle." [2 marks]
→ Releases CO₂ stored in fossil fuels (1) + increases atmospheric CO₂ (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-carbon-cycle

## SECTION 7.7 — Human Effects on Biodiversity

---

**B7-23** | Topic: Human Effects | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 ways** humans **reduce biodiversity**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- **Deforestation** — destroys habitats, releases CO₂
- **Pollution** of water with fertilisers and sewage
- **Using land** for building, farming and quarrying
- **Destroying peat bogs** — releases CO₂, removes habitat

Hint: Deforestation, Pollution, Land use, Peat destruction — DPLP

Exam Q: "Give two ways humans reduce biodiversity." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from the list = 2 marks.

Notes link: /notes/b7-human-effects

---

**B7-24** | Topic: Human Effects | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **deforestation** affects the environment. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Burning or decomposing trees **releases CO₂**
- **Fewer trees** means less CO₂ removed from the atmosphere
- **Loss of habitat** reduces biodiversity

Hint: More CO₂ released + less removed + habitats lost = bad for environment

Exam Q: "Describe two effects of deforestation on the environment." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: releases CO₂ / less CO₂ absorbed / loss of biodiversity / habitat loss.

Notes link: /notes/b7-human-effects

---

**B7-25** | Topic: Human Effects | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **water pollution** with fertilisers affects ecosystems. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Fertilisers cause rapid **algae growth** in water (eutrophication)
- Algae **block light** — plants below die
- Bacteria **decompose** dead plants — using up **oxygen** — other organisms die

Hint: Fertiliser → algae bloom → blocks light → plants die → bacteria use oxygen → animals die

Exam Q: "Describe the effects of fertiliser run-off on aquatic ecosystems." [3 marks]
→ Algae growth (1) + blocks light / plants die (1) + bacteria use oxygen / animals die (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-human-effects

---

**B7-26** | Topic: Human Effects | Tier: F

Q: From what you have learnt, give **3 facts** about how **global warming** affects biodiversity. *(3 facts)*

A:
- **Sea levels rise** — habitats lost
- Animals and plants can **no longer survive** in their current area
- **Migration patterns** of animals change

Hint: Hotter planet → habitats change or disappear → species affected

Exam Q: "Give two effects of global warming on biodiversity." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: sea level rise / habitat loss / species can't survive / migration changes.

Notes link: /notes/b7-human-effects

---

**B7-27** | Topic: Human Effects | Tier: H

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **toxic chemicals** affect food chains. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Pesticides and toxic chemicals are **washed into rivers** by rain
- They **accumulate** in animals — more at each level of the food chain
- **Top predators** get the highest concentration — they die or fail to breed

Hint: Toxins build up at each level. Top predators get the most. Called bioaccumulation.

Exam Q: "Describe how toxic chemicals in water affect organisms higher up the food chain." [3 marks]
→ Chemicals accumulate (1) + more at each trophic level (1) + top predators most affected (1).

## SECTION 7.9 — Measuring Biodiversity (Required Practical)

---

**B7-30** | Topic: Measuring Biodiversity | Tier: F

Q: What is the purpose of **random sampling** using quadrats? *(2 facts)*

A:
- To **estimate** the size of a population in an area
- Without counting **every** individual organism

Hint: Quadrats sample a small area — results used to estimate the whole population

Exam Q: "Describe why random sampling is used to estimate population size." [2 marks]
→ To estimate population (1) + without counting every organism (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-required-practical

---

**B7-31** | Topic: Measuring Biodiversity | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** of **random sampling** using quadrats. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Assign **co-ordinates** to the area being sampled
- Use **random numbers** to choose co-ordinates
- Place the **quadrat** and count organisms present
- Calculate the **mean** number of organisms per quadrat

Hint: Co-ordinates → random → place quadrat → count → mean

Exam Q: "Describe how to carry out random sampling using quadrats." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

Notes link: /notes/b7-required-practical

---

**B7-32** | Topic: Measuring Biodiversity | Tier: F

Q: What is a **transect** and when is it used? *(3 facts)*

A:
- A **line** placed across a habitat
- Quadrats placed at **set distances** along the line
- Used to see how **populations change** over a distance

Hint: Transect = line across the habitat. Shows gradual change.

Exam Q: "Describe how a transect is used to study populations." [3 marks]
→ Line placed across habitat (1) + quadrats at set distances (1) + shows how populations change over distance (1).

Notes link: /notes/b7-required-practical

---

**B7-33** | Topic: Measuring Biodiversity | Tier: F

Q: Why is it important that sampling is **random**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Removes **bias** from results
- Makes the results more **representative** of the whole area

Hint: Random = fair sample = results you can trust

Exam Q: "Describe why it is important that sampling is random." [2 marks]
→ Removes bias (1) + results are representative of the whole area (1).
