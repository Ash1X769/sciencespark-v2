## SECTION 6.1 — Sexual and Asexual Reproduction

---

**B6-01** | Topic: Reproduction | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **sexual reproduction**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Involves the **fusion** of two **gametes**
- Produces offspring with **variation**
- Offspring are **not** genetically identical to parents

Hint: Two parents + gametes + variation = sexual reproduction

Exam Q: "Give two features of sexual reproduction." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: involves gametes / produces variation / two parents needed.

---

**B6-02** | Topic: Reproduction | Tier: F

Q: What is a **gamete**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- A **sex cell**
- Contains **half** the genetic information of a body cell
- Examples: **sperm** and **egg** in animals / **pollen** in plants

Hint: Half the chromosomes. Sperm + egg = gametes.

Exam Q: "What is a gamete?" [2 marks]
→ Sex cell (1) + contains half the genetic information (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-reproduction

---

**B6-03** | Topic: Reproduction | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **asexual reproduction**. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Involves **one parent** only
- **No** gametes are involved
- Offspring are **genetically identical** clones — only **mitosis** involved

Hint: One parent + no gametes + identical clones = asexual reproduction

Exam Q: "Give two features of asexual reproduction." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: one parent / no gametes / genetically identical offspring / only mitosis.

Notes link: /notes/b6-reproduction

---

**B6-04** | Topic: Reproduction | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 advantages** and **2 disadvantages** of **sexual reproduction**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Advantage: produces **variation** — offspring more likely to survive environmental changes
- Advantage: better chance of **surviving disease**
- Disadvantage: requires a **mate**
- Disadvantage: **slower** at producing offspring

Hint: Variation = good for survival. But needs two parents and takes time.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage and one disadvantage of sexual reproduction." [2 marks]
→ Advantage: produces variation (1). Disadvantage: requires a mate / slower (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-reproduction

---

**B6-05** | Topic: Reproduction | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 advantages** and **2 disadvantages** of **asexual reproduction**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Advantage: produces **lots of offspring quickly**
- Advantage: **no mate** needed
- Disadvantage: offspring are **less likely to survive** environmental changes or disease
- Disadvantage: **no variation** in offspring

Hint: Fast and easy, but no variation = vulnerable to change.

Exam Q: "Give one advantage and one disadvantage of asexual reproduction." [2 marks]
→ Advantage: fast / no mate needed (1). Disadvantage: no variation / less likely to survive change (1).

## SECTION 6.2 — Meiosis

---

**B6-06** | Topic: Meiosis | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about **meiosis**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Produces **gametes** (sex cells)
- Produces **4** daughter cells
- Daughter cells are **genetically different** from each other
- Each daughter cell has **half** the number of chromosomes

Hint: 4 cells, all different, all with half the chromosomes

Exam Q: "Give two features of meiosis." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: produces 4 cells / cells are genetically different / half the chromosomes / produces gametes.

Notes link: /notes/b6-meiosis

---

**B6-07** | Topic: Meiosis | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 differences** between **mitosis** and **meiosis**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Mitosis produces **2** cells — meiosis produces **4**
- Mitosis cells are **genetically identical** — meiosis cells are **different**
- Mitosis cells have the **full** number of chromosomes — meiosis cells have **half**
- Mitosis is for **growth and repair** — meiosis is for **gamete production**

Hint: Mitosis = 2 identical. Meiosis = 4 different, half chromosomes.

Exam Q: "Give two differences between mitosis and meiosis." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from the bullets above. Must be comparative.

Notes link: /notes/b6-meiosis

---

**B6-08** | Topic: Meiosis | Tier: F

Q: What is **fertilisation**? Give **2 facts**. *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **fusion** of two gametes
- **Restores** the full number of chromosomes

Hint: Sperm + egg → full set of chromosomes restored

Exam Q: "What happens during fertilisation?" [2 marks]
→ Gametes fuse (1) + full chromosome number restored (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-meiosis

## SECTION 6.3 — Genetics Key Terms

---

**B6-09** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: F

Q: What is **DNA**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- The **genetic material** of a cell
- A **polymer** made of two strands forming a **double helix**
- Makes up **chromosomes**

Hint: Double helix shape. Carries all genetic instructions.

Exam Q: "Describe the structure of DNA." [2 marks]
→ Double helix (1) + made of two strands / polymer (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetics

---

**B6-10** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: F

Q: What is a **gene**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- A small section of **DNA** on a **chromosome**
- Codes for a particular sequence of **amino acids**
- Determines which **protein** is made

Hint: Gene = one instruction. Codes for one protein.

Exam Q: "What is a gene?" [2 marks]
→ Section of DNA (1) + codes for a sequence of amino acids / protein (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetics

---

**B6-11** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: F

Q: What is a **chromosome**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- A long **coil of DNA**
- Found in the **nucleus**
- Human body cells have **46** chromosomes (23 pairs)

Hint: Coiled DNA in the nucleus. Humans have 46.

Exam Q: "How many chromosomes does a human body cell contain?" [1 mark]
→ 46 / 23 pairs.

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetics

---

**B6-12** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: H

Q: What is the **genome**? *(1 fact)*

A:
- The **entire genetic material** of an organism

Hint: Genome = ALL the genes. Every instruction the organism has.

Exam Q: "What is meant by the genome of an organism?" [1 mark]
→ The entire genetic material of that organism.

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetics

---

**B6-13** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: F

Q: What is an **allele**? *(1 fact)*

A:
- A **different version** of the same **gene**

Hint: Same gene, different version. Like different flavours of the same instruction.

Exam Q: "What is an allele?" [1 mark]
→ A different version of the same gene.

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetics

---

**B6-14** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between a **dominant** and a **recessive** allele? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **dominant** allele is always **expressed** — only **one copy** is needed
- A **recessive** allele is only expressed when **two copies** are present

Hint: Dominant = one copy enough. Recessive = need two copies.

Exam Q: "What is the difference between a dominant and a recessive allele?" [2 marks]
→ Dominant expressed with one copy (1) + recessive only expressed with two copies (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetics

---

**B6-15** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **homozygous** and **heterozygous**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Homozygous** — both alleles for a gene are the **same** (e.g. BB or bb)
- **Heterozygous** — both alleles for a gene are **different** (e.g. Bb)

Hint: Homo = same. Hetero = different.

Exam Q: "What is meant by homozygous?" [1 mark]
→ Both alleles for a gene are the same.

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetics

---

**B6-16** | Topic: Genetics | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **genotype** and **phenotype**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Genotype** — the **alleles** an organism has for a gene (e.g. Bb)
- **Phenotype** — the **physical feature** that is expressed (e.g. brown eyes)

Hint: Genotype = genes you have. Phenotype = what you look like.

Exam Q: "What is the difference between genotype and phenotype?" [2 marks]
→ Genotype = alleles present (1) + phenotype = physical characteristic expressed (1).

## SECTION 6.4 — Genetic Diseases

---

**B6-17** | Topic: Genetic Diseases | Tier: F

Q: From what you have learnt, give **4 facts** about **polydactyly**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- Causes **extra fingers or toes**
- Caused by a **dominant** allele
- Only **one copy** of the allele is needed to have the condition
- Genotype of affected person: **PP or Pp**

Hint: Dominant = one copy enough. Extra digits = polydactyly.

Exam Q: "A person has polydactyly. Explain why their child may also have polydactyly." [2 marks]
→ Caused by dominant allele (1) + only one copy needed / 50% chance of passing on (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetic-diseases

---

**B6-18** | Topic: Genetic Diseases | Tier: F

Q: From what you have learnt, give **4 facts** about **cystic fibrosis**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- A disorder of **cell membranes**
- Causes **sticky mucus** to build up in the lungs
- Caused by a **recessive** allele
- **Two copies** of the allele are needed — carriers (Cc) don't show symptoms

Hint: Recessive = need two copies. Cc = carrier. cc = affected.

Exam Q: "Describe why two unaffected parents can have a child with cystic fibrosis." [3 marks]
→ Caused by recessive allele (1) + parents are carriers / Cc (1) + 25% chance of cc offspring (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetic-diseases

---

**B6-19** | Topic: Genetic Diseases | Tier: F

Q: What is a **carrier**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- A person who has **one recessive allele** for a condition (e.g. Cc)
- They **don't show** the symptoms
- They **can pass** the allele on to their children

Hint: Cc = carrier. Has the allele but doesn't show it.

Exam Q: "What is meant by a carrier?" [2 marks]
→ Has one recessive allele (1) + doesn't show the condition but can pass it on (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetic-diseases

## SECTION 6.5 — Sex Determination

---

**B6-20** | Topic: Sex Determination | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about how **sex is determined** in humans. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Females have **XX** sex chromosomes
- Males have **XY** sex chromosomes
- The **father** determines the sex — sperm carries either X or Y

Hint: XX = female, XY = male. Dad decides.

Exam Q: "Describe how the sex of a baby is determined." [3 marks]
→ XX = female / XY = male (1) + father's sperm carries X or Y (1) + 50% chance of each sex (1).

## SECTION 6.7 — Natural Selection and Evolution

---

**B6-23** | Topic: Natural Selection | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** of **natural selection**. *(4 facts)*

A:
- There is **variation** in a population caused by **mutations**
- There is **competition** for resources (e.g. food)
- The **best adapted** organisms survive and **breed**
- They pass on their **alleles** — so over time better adapted alleles **increase** in the population

Hint: Variation → competition → survival of fittest → passed on → population changes

Exam Q: "Describe how natural selection leads to evolution." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

Notes link: /notes/b6-natural-selection

---

**B6-24** | Topic: Natural Selection | Tier: F

Q: What is **evolution**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **change in the genes** of a population **over time**
- Occurs through **natural selection**

Hint: Populations slowly change over generations. Natural selection drives it.

Exam Q: "What is evolution?" [2 marks]
→ Change in genes of a population (1) + over time / through natural selection (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-evolution

---

**B6-25** | Topic: Natural Selection | Tier: H

Q: What is **speciation**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Occurs when two populations become so **different**
- They can **no longer breed** to produce **fertile** offspring
- Two separate **species** are formed

Hint: Isolated + different selection pressures + time = new species

Exam Q: "What is speciation?" [2 marks]
→ Populations can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring (1) + two new species formed (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-evolution

- Choose offspring with the **desired characteristic** and breed them again
- Repeat over **many generations**

## SECTION 6.9 — Genetic Engineering

---

**B6-30** | Topic: Genetic Engineering | Tier: F

Q: What is **genetic engineering**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- Modifying the **genome** of an organism
- By introducing a **gene** from another organism
- To give a **desired characteristic**

Hint: Cut out a useful gene. Put it into another organism. That organism now has the new trait.

Exam Q: "What is genetic engineering?" [2 marks]
→ Modifying the genome (1) + by inserting a gene from another organism (1).

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetic-engineering

---

**B6-31** | Topic: Genetic Engineering | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** of the **genetic engineering** process. *(4 facts)*

A:
- The desired **gene is cut out** using enzymes
- The gene is inserted into a **vector** (bacterial plasmid or virus)
- The vector **carries the gene** into the target organism's cells
- The organism develops with the **desired characteristic**

Hint: Cut → vector → insert → new characteristic

Exam Q: "Describe how genetic engineering is carried out." [4 marks]
→ One mark per bullet above.

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetic-engineering

---

**B6-32** | Topic: Genetic Engineering | Tier: F

Q: Give **2 examples** of **genetic engineering** in use. *(2 facts)*

A:
- Bacterial cells have the **human insulin gene** inserted — they produce insulin for diabetics
- Crop plants have genes inserted that make them **resistant to disease, insects or herbicides**

Hint: Insulin bacteria + disease-resistant GM crops = two key examples

Exam Q: "Give one example of genetic engineering." [1 mark]
→ Any 1 from: bacterial insulin production / GM disease-resistant crops.

Notes link: /notes/b6-genetic-engineering

---

**B6-33** | Topic: Genetic Engineering | Tier: F

Q: What is a **vector** in genetic engineering? *(2 facts)*

A:
- Something that **carries a gene** into another organism
- Examples: **bacterial plasmid** or **virus**

Hint: Vector = carrier. Delivers the gene to where it's needed.

Exam Q: "What is a vector in genetic engineering?" [2 marks]
→ Carries a gene into another organism (1) + example: plasmid or virus (1).
