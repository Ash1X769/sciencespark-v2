## SECTION 5.1 — Key Terms

---

**P5-01** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between a **scalar** and a **vector**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Scalar** — has **magnitude (size) only** — e.g. speed, distance, mass, temperature
- **Vector** — has both **magnitude and direction** — e.g. velocity, displacement, force, acceleration

Hint: Scalar = size only. Vector = size AND direction. Forces are always vectors.

Exam Q: "State one scalar quantity and one vector quantity." [2 marks]
→ Scalar: any 1 from speed / distance / mass / temperature (1). Vector: any 1 from velocity / force / acceleration / displacement (1).

---

**P5-02** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **contact** and **non-contact** forces? Give examples. *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Contact forces** — between objects that are **touching** (e.g. friction, air resistance, tension, normal contact force)
- **Non-contact forces** — between objects that are **separate** (e.g. gravity, magnetic force, electrostatic force)

Hint: Contact = touching. Non-contact = acting at a distance.

Exam Q: "Give one example of a contact force and one example of a non-contact force." [2 marks]
→ Contact: friction / tension / air resistance (1). Non-contact: gravity / magnetic / electrostatic (1).

Notes link: /notes/p5-key-terms

---

**P5-03** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is a **resultant force**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- A **single force** that has the **same effect** as all the forces acting on an object combined
- If resultant force is **zero**, the object remains stationary or moves at **constant velocity**

Hint: Add up all forces on an object (including direction) = resultant force.

Exam Q: "What is meant by a resultant force?" [2 marks]
→ Single force with same effect as all forces acting (1) + takes into account magnitude and direction (1).

Notes link: /notes/p5-key-terms

---

**P5-04** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **work done**? Give the equation. *(3 facts)*

A:
- Work is done when a **force moves an object** through a **distance**
- **Work done = force × distance** → **W = Fs**
- Units: work in **J**, force in **N**, distance in **m**

Hint: W = Fs. Force times distance. 1 joule = 1 newton × 1 metre.

Exam Q: "Calculate the work done when a 50 N force moves an object 4 m." [2 marks]
→ W = 50 × 4 = **200 J**. Show working.

## SECTION 5.2 — Key Equations

---

**P5-05** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation for **weight**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Weight = mass × gravitational field strength**
- **W = mg**
- Units: weight in **N**, mass in **kg**, g = **10 N/kg** on Earth

Hint: W = mg. Weight is a force (in newtons). Mass is in kg.

Exam Q: "Calculate the weight of a 5 kg object on Earth. [g = 10 N/kg]" [2 marks]
→ W = 5 × 10 = **50 N**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p5-equations

---

**P5-06** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is **Hooke's Law**? Give the equation. *(3 facts)*

A:
- The **extension** of a spring is **directly proportional** to the **force** applied — up to the **limit of proportionality**
- **Force = spring constant × extension** → **F = ke**
- Units: force in **N**, spring constant in **N/m**, extension in **m**

Hint: F = ke. Stiffer spring = higher k. Double the force = double the extension (up to the limit).

Exam Q: "A spring with a spring constant of 25 N/m is extended by 0.04 m. Calculate the force applied." [2 marks]
→ F = 25 × 0.04 = **1 N**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p5-equations

---

**P5-07** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation relating **distance, speed and time**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Distance = speed × time** → **s = vt**
- Units: distance in **m**, speed in **m/s**, time in **s**

Hint: s = vt. Speed × time = distance. The speed triangle.

Exam Q: "Calculate the distance travelled by an object moving at 4 m/s for 6 s." [2 marks]
→ s = 4 × 6 = **24 m**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p5-equations

---

**P5-08** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is the equation for **acceleration**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time taken**
- **a = Δv/t**
- Units: acceleration in **m/s²**, velocity in **m/s**, time in **s**

Hint: a = Δv/t. How quickly velocity changes. Negative acceleration = deceleration.

Exam Q: "A car increases its velocity from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 s. Calculate its acceleration." [2 marks]
→ a = (25 − 5) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = **5 m/s²**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p5-equations

---

**P5-09** | Topic: Equations | Tier: F

Q: What is **Newton's Second Law** equation? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Resultant force = mass × acceleration**
- **F = ma**
- Units: force in **N**, mass in **kg**, acceleration in **m/s²**

Hint: F = ma. Greater force OR less mass = greater acceleration.

Exam Q: "Calculate the resultant force needed to accelerate a 4 kg object at 3 m/s²." [2 marks]
→ F = 4 × 3 = **12 N**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p5-equations

---

**P5-10** | Topic: Equations | Tier: H

Q: What is the equation for **momentum**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Momentum = mass × velocity**
- **p = mv**
- Units: momentum in **kg m/s**, mass in **kg**, velocity in **m/s**

Hint: p = mv. Heavier and faster = more momentum.

Exam Q: "Calculate the momentum of a 0.5 kg ball moving at 6 m/s." [2 marks]
→ p = 0.5 × 6 = **3 kg m/s**. Show working.

Notes link: /notes/p5-equations

## SECTION 5.3 — Newton's Laws

---

**P5-11** | Topic: Newton's Laws | Tier: F

Q: State **Newton's First Law** of motion. *(2 facts)*

A:
- An object will remain **stationary** or continue at **constant velocity**
- Unless acted on by a **resultant force**

Hint: No resultant force = no change in motion. Still stays still. Moving stays moving.

Exam Q: "State Newton's First Law of motion." [2 marks]
→ Object remains stationary or moves at constant velocity (1) + unless a resultant force acts on it (1).

Notes link: /notes/p5-newton

---

**P5-12** | Topic: Newton's Laws | Tier: F

Q: State **Newton's Second Law** of motion. *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **acceleration** of an object is **proportional** to the **resultant force** acting on it
- And **inversely proportional** to its **mass** — F = ma

Hint: More force = more acceleration. More mass = less acceleration. F = ma.

Exam Q: "State Newton's Second Law." [1 mark]
→ F = ma / resultant force = mass × acceleration.

Notes link: /notes/p5-newton

---

**P5-13** | Topic: Newton's Laws | Tier: F

Q: State **Newton's Third Law** of motion. *(2 facts)*

A:
- Whenever two objects interact, the forces they exert on each other are **equal in size**
- And act in **opposite directions** — on **different** objects

Hint: Action and reaction — equal and opposite. Always on DIFFERENT objects.

Exam Q: "State Newton's Third Law." [1 mark]
→ For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction / forces between two objects are equal and opposite.

Notes link: /notes/p5-newton

---

**P5-14** | Topic: Newton's Laws | Tier: H

Q: What is **inertia**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The tendency of objects to **continue** in their state of rest or **uniform motion**
- Objects with **greater mass** have greater inertia — harder to change their motion

Hint: Inertia = resistance to change in motion. Heavy objects are harder to start or stop.

Exam Q: "What is inertia?" [1 mark]
→ The tendency of an object to remain in its state of rest or uniform motion.

Notes link: /notes/p5-newton

## SECTION 5.4 — Elasticity

---

**P5-15** | Topic: Elasticity | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **elastic** and **inelastic deformation**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Elastic deformation** — the object **returns** to its original shape when force is removed
- **Inelastic deformation** — the object is **permanently** changed — doesn't return to original shape

Hint: Elastic = bounces back. Inelastic = permanently stretched/bent.

Exam Q: "What is meant by elastic deformation?" [1 mark]
→ The object returns to its original shape when the force is removed.

Notes link: /notes/p5-elasticity

---

**P5-16** | Topic: Elasticity | Tier: F

Q: What is the **limit of proportionality**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The point beyond which **Hooke's Law no longer applies**
- Beyond this point, a **force-extension graph** becomes **curved** — extension is no longer proportional to force

Hint: Past the limit = spring doesn't obey Hooke's Law. Graph curves upward.

Exam Q: "What is meant by the limit of proportionality?" [2 marks]
→ Point beyond which extension is no longer proportional to force / Hooke's Law no longer applies (1) + graph becomes curved beyond this point (1).

Notes link: /notes/p5-elasticity

## SECTION 5.5 — Motion Graphs

---

**P5-17** | Topic: Motion Graphs | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about **distance-time graphs**.

A:
- **Horizontal line** = stationary (not moving)
- **Straight line with positive gradient** = constant speed
- **Curved line (steeper)** = accelerating
- The **gradient** of the line = **speed**

Hint: Flat = stopped. Straight = constant speed. Curved = accelerating. Gradient = speed.

Exam Q: "What does the gradient of a distance-time graph represent?" [1 mark]
→ Speed.

Notes link: /notes/p5-motion-graphs

---

**P5-18** | Topic: Motion Graphs | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 facts** about **velocity-time graphs**.

A:
- **Horizontal line** = constant velocity (no acceleration)
- **Straight line with positive gradient** = constant acceleration
- **Straight line with negative gradient** = constant deceleration
- The **gradient** = **acceleration**; the **area under the graph** = **distance travelled**

Hint: Gradient = acceleration. Area under = distance. Flat = constant speed.

Exam Q: "What does the area under a velocity-time graph represent?" [1 mark]
→ Distance travelled.

Notes link: /notes/p5-motion-graphs

## SECTION 5.6 — Stopping Distance

---

**P5-19** | Topic: Stopping Distance | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **stopping distance**.

A:
- **Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance**
- **Thinking distance** — distance travelled during the driver's **reaction time**
- **Braking distance** — distance travelled while the **brakes are applied**

Hint: Total stopping distance = thinking + braking. Both must be considered.

Exam Q: "What is meant by stopping distance?" [2 marks]
→ Thinking distance + braking distance (1) + total distance from when hazard seen to when vehicle stops (1).

Notes link: /notes/p5-stopping-distance

---

**P5-20** | Topic: Stopping Distance | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 factors** that affect **thinking distance** and **3 factors** that affect **braking distance**.

A:
- **Thinking distance** increases with: **tiredness**, **alcohol or drugs**, **distractions** (longer reaction time)
- **Braking distance** increases with: higher **speed**, poor **weather** (ice/rain), worn **brakes or tyres**

Hint: Thinking = reaction time factors (driver). Braking = road/vehicle/speed factors.

Exam Q: "Give two factors that increase braking distance." [2 marks]
→ Any 2 from: high speed / icy or wet roads / worn brakes or tyres.

Notes link: /notes/p5-stopping-distance

## SECTION 5.7 — Terminal Velocity

---

**P5-21** | Topic: Terminal Velocity | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **terminal velocity**.

A:
- The **maximum speed** of a falling object
- Reached when **driving force** (e.g. gravity) equals **resistive forces** (e.g. air resistance)
- At terminal velocity, **resultant force = 0** → **no acceleration**

Hint: Terminal velocity = balanced forces. Gravity down = air resistance up. No acceleration.

Exam Q: "Explain why a skydiver reaches a terminal velocity." [3 marks]
→ Weight / gravity acts downward (1) + air resistance increases as speed increases (1) + when air resistance = weight / resultant force = 0 / no more acceleration (1).
