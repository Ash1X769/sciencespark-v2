## SECTION 3.1 — Key Terms

---

**P3-01** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **density**? Give the equation. *(3 facts)*

A:
- A measure of how much **mass** is packed into a given **volume**
- **Density = mass ÷ volume**
- **ρ = m/V**
- Units: density in **kg/m³**, mass in **kg**, volume in **m³**

Hint: Dense = lots of mass in a small space. Solids are usually denser than liquids or gases.

Exam Q: "Calculate the density of an object with a mass of 200 g and a volume of 40 cm³." [2 marks]
→ ρ = 200 ÷ 40 = **5 g/cm³**. Show working.

**P3-02** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What is **internal energy**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **total kinetic energy and potential energy** of all the particles in a system
- Can be increased by **heating** or doing **work** on the system

Hint: Internal energy = all the kinetic + potential energy of every particle inside the object.

Exam Q: "What is internal energy?" [2 marks]
→ Total kinetic and potential energy (1) + of all the particles in the system (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-internal-energy

---

**P3-03** | Topic: Key Terms | Tier: F

Q: What does **temperature** measure? *(2 facts)*

A:
- The **average kinetic energy** of the particles in a substance
- Higher temperature = particles move **faster** on average

Hint: Temperature = average kinetic energy of particles. Not the same as internal energy.

Exam Q: "What does temperature measure?" [1 mark]
→ Average kinetic energy of the particles.

## SECTION 3.2 — States of Matter

---

**P3-04** | Topic: States of Matter | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about particles in a **solid**.

A:
- Particles are held in **fixed positions** in a regular arrangement
- They **vibrate** about their fixed position but don't move freely
- **Closely packed** together — very little space between particles

Hint: Fixed, vibrating, close. Solids hold their shape.

Exam Q: "Describe the arrangement and motion of particles in a solid." [2 marks]
→ Fixed positions / regular arrangement (1) + vibrate about fixed position (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-states

---

**P3-05** | Topic: States of Matter | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about particles in a **liquid**.

A:
- Particles are **touching** but can **move around** each other
- **No regular arrangement** — randomly distributed
- Have **more energy** than solid particles

Hint: Close but free to move. Liquids flow and take the shape of their container.

Exam Q: "Describe the arrangement and motion of particles in a liquid." [2 marks]
→ Close together / touching (1) + can move around each other / no fixed positions (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-states

---

**P3-06** | Topic: States of Matter | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about particles in a **gas**.

A:
- Particles are **widely spread** — lots of empty space between them
- Move **randomly** and **quickly** in all directions
- Have **much more energy** than liquid or solid particles

Hint: Far apart, moving fast, random. Gases expand to fill their container.

Exam Q: "Describe the arrangement and motion of particles in a gas." [2 marks]
→ Far apart / widely spaced (1) + move randomly / quickly in all directions (1).

## SECTION 3.3 — Changes of State

---

**P3-07** | Topic: Changes of State | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **changes of state**.

A:
- When a substance changes from one state to another (e.g. solid → liquid = **melting**)
- During a change of state, temperature **does not change** — energy changes the **arrangement** of particles
- Changes of state are **physical** changes — they can be reversed

Hint: Melting, freezing, evaporating, condensing — all changes of state. Temperature stays constant during change.

Exam Q: "Explain why the temperature does not change when a substance is melting." [2 marks]
→ Energy is used to weaken / break forces between particles (1) + not to increase kinetic energy / temperature (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-changes-of-state

---

**P3-08** | Topic: Changes of State | Tier: F

Q: What is **specific latent heat**? *(3 facts)*

A:
- The energy needed to **change the state** of **1 kg** of a substance
- With **no change in temperature**
- Units: **J/kg**

Hint: Latent = hidden. Energy goes in but temperature doesn't change — it's changing the state instead.

Exam Q: "What is specific latent heat?" [2 marks]
→ Energy needed to change state of 1 kg (1) + with no change in temperature (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-latent-heat

---

**P3-09** | Topic: Changes of State | Tier: F

Q: What is the difference between **latent heat of fusion** and **latent heat of vaporisation**? *(2 facts)*

A:
- **Latent heat of fusion** — energy needed to change state from **solid to liquid** (melting)
- **Latent heat of vaporisation** — energy needed to change state from **liquid to vapour** (boiling)

Hint: Fusion = melting. Vaporisation = boiling. Vaporisation always requires more energy than fusion.

Exam Q: "What is the latent heat of fusion of a substance?" [1 mark]
→ Energy needed to change 1 kg from solid to liquid / at constant temperature.

Notes link: /notes/p3-latent-heat

## SECTION 3.4 — The Heating Curve

---

**P3-10** | Topic: Heating Curve | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about what happens during **melting** on a heating curve.

A:
- Temperature **stays constant** during melting
- Energy supplied is used to **weaken the forces** between particles (increasing potential energy)
- **Internal energy** increases — but kinetic energy (and temperature) doesn't

Hint: Flat section on graph = melting. Temperature stays the same while bonds break.

Exam Q: "Use the particle model to explain what happens during melting." [3 marks]
→ Temperature stays constant (1) + energy used to weaken / overcome forces between particles (1) + potential energy of particles increases (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-heating-curve

---

**P3-11** | Topic: Heating Curve | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about what happens during **heating of a solid** on a heating curve.

A:
- Temperature **increases** as energy is added
- Particles **vibrate more** — kinetic energy increases
- **Internal energy** increases

Hint: Rising section = solid heating up. Particles vibrate faster.

Exam Q: "Describe what happens to particles in a solid as it is heated." [2 marks]
→ Particles vibrate more / kinetic energy increases (1) + temperature increases / internal energy increases (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-heating-curve

## SECTION 3.5 — Gas Pressure

---

**P3-12** | Topic: Gas Pressure | Tier: F

Q: Give **3 facts** about **gas pressure**.

A:
- Caused by gas particles **colliding** with the walls of the container
- Each collision exerts a **force** on the wall
- As **temperature increases**, particles move faster → more frequent and more forceful collisions → pressure **increases**

Hint: More/faster collisions = higher pressure. Hot gas in closed container = higher pressure.

Exam Q: "Explain why increasing the temperature of a gas in a sealed container increases the pressure." [3 marks]
→ Particles move faster (1) + collide more frequently / with more force (1) + pressure increases (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-gas-pressure

---

**P3-13** | Topic: Gas Pressure | Tier: H

Q: Give **2 facts** about the relationship between **pressure and volume** of a gas at constant temperature.

A:
- Decreasing the volume **increases** the pressure — particles collide more frequently
- The relationship is **inversely proportional**: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Hint: Squash a gas = higher pressure. Double the pressure = half the volume.

Exam Q: "Explain what happens to the pressure of a gas when the volume is decreased at constant temperature." [2 marks]
→ Particles are closer together (1) + collide with walls more frequently / pressure increases (1).

Notes link: /notes/p3-gas-pressure

## SECTION 3.6 — Specific Heat Capacity (Required Practical)

---

**P3-14** | Topic: Required Practical | Tier: F

Q: Give **4 steps** of the **specific heat capacity** required practical.

A:
- Measure the **mass** of the material using a balance
- Use a **thermometer** to record the starting temperature
- Heat with an **immersion heater** — record energy supplied using a **joulemeter**
- Record the **temperature change** and calculate SHC using **E = mcΔθ**

Hint: Mass → start temp → heat (record energy) → temp change → calculate SHC.

Exam Q: "Describe how to measure the specific heat capacity of a metal block." [4 marks]
→ Measure mass (1) + record start temperature (1) + heat with immersion heater / measure energy (1) + record temperature change / calculate using E = mcΔθ (1).
